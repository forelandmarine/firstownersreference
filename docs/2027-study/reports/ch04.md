# Chapter 04, The acquisition process: editing report

Date: 14 November 2026. Edits file: docs/2027-study/edits/ch04.json (39 edits: 24 fact, 9 line, 3 repetition, 3 structure). I validated the file with `scripts/apply-edits.py --dry-run`, and also applied it to a scratch copy of the content. Every `old` occurs once, and both deletions and the ch09 insertion match one paragraph each. No em dashes are introduced. The essay goes from 2,265 to about 2,325 words. About 250 words move to ch09, and the Spanish IPR correction adds about 100.

## Overall assessment

This is the book's clearest example of applying the ch03 argument to a real sequence, and most of it stands. The work fell into four areas:
- the Spanish IPR error (C9), with ch04 made the single home of the VAT regimes;
- a compliance figure that overlapped with ch07 (C12);
- advice on budget and brokers that did not match the written-disclosure standard;
- a run of unsourced frequency claims and closers.

Every practical instruction is kept: keep the budget figure with the adviser, use a buyer-paid surveyor, resist an early close, get separate VAT advice, and hold a full technical handover. Each now gives its reason, and none goes beyond the evidence.

## Factual concerns and status

**C9, Spanish IPR (para 23, data spread). Resolved.** The essay said IPR "suspends 21 percent VAT on refit works ... within an 18-month admission window". It also counted IPR among "three legitimate alternatives" to VAT on purchase, then listed four routes. The new para 22 says three routes reduce VAT on the yacht and a fourth applies to refit. Para 23 now matches ch06 70 to 71 and the glossary entry `spanish-ipr`:
- the works are exempt from the 21 percent VAT, provided the procedure is followed through to departure from the EU;
- authorisation must be in place before the works begin;
- time under inward processing does not count against the 18-month temporary admission period;
- a pointer to ch06 covers the mechanics and where compliance rests.

In the data spread, the "Three legitimate alternatives" heading is corrected, and the IPR row's effect and conditions are rewritten. "Extendable to 24" months is removed because nothing in the book supports it. The glossary needed no change. The Maltby answer in ch06 ("VAT suspended under the customs procedure") is published as given. It is consistent if read as the suspension of import VAT on the yacht itself.

**C12, compliance cost (para 30). Resolved for ch04. Residual flagged for ch07.** The ch04 data spread already breaks the EUR 50,000 to 150,000 down:

| Item | EUR a year |
|---|---|
| Tax compliance and structuring | 25,000 to 70,000 |
| Owning company maintenance | 10,000 to 40,000 |
| Flag administration and class society fees | 15,000 to 40,000 |
| Total | 50,000 to 150,000 |

Tax, structuring and the owning company together come to 35,000 to 110,000. Para 30 now gives both parts. It says flag and class fees also sit inside ch07 23's EUR 80,000 to 200,000 (ISM, MLC, flag, class, management compliance services) and should be counted once. The data-spread caption and the checklist item are aligned.

The ch01 10 figure (regulatory and class compliance at 2 to 3 percent of operating cost) cannot be reconciled from the text, because the book gives no 40m operating budget. As an illustration only, on an assumed EUR 2 to 4 million budget, 2 to 3 percent is EUR 40,000 to 120,000. ch07's 80,000 to 200,000 would be about 2 to 10 percent of the same budget. The gap is plausibly explained by two things, both for the ch07 pass to settle:
- ch07 23 includes the management company's compliance services, which ch01 counts under management fees (3 to 5 percent).
- It applies ISM and MLC to a 40m yacht, which ch07 20 says is usually outside both when used privately.

**C27, AMLR (para 31). Resolved.** The wording now follows ch03 9: traders carry out customer due diligence and report sales of non-commercial watercraft at EUR 7.5 million or more to the national financial intelligence unit, with a pointer to chapter 3.

## Significant line changes

- **Para 3 (disclosure standard).** "Do not disclose budget to anyone paid contingent on a sale closing" becomes "keep the budget figure with the independent adviser". The paragraph now gives the reason. Brokers on both sides are usually paid from the seller's commission, which rises with the price (ch03). Written disclosure tells the buyer how a broker is paid, but a budget figure can still become the floor of the negotiation. The advice is as firm as before and now rests on the ch03 evidence. The checklist item and its detail are aligned.
- **Para 10.** "Treat a broker-recommended surveyor as unverified" becomes advice to ask in writing about any work for the broker or seller, then instruct and pay the surveyor directly. That matches ch09 question 8.

- **Para 38** (fairness flag 10). "Almost always the seller's interest ... costs nothing" becomes: an early close suits the seller and the parties paid on completion, the buyer carries whatever was not checked, and three weeks cost little against what they can find.
