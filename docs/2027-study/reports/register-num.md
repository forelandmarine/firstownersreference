14 November 2026. Register: docs/2027-study/register-num.csv (25 rows). Edits: docs/2027-study/edits/register-num.json (13 Ready edits; dry run passes). Methodology record: docs/2027-study/methodology-record.md.

## What changed (Ready)

**Operating cost (item 1).** The two 50 m figures are both legitimate but measure different things, and neither page said so. The running cost calculator is calibrated to Christie Yachts' published table (45 to 50 m, 500 GT, 11 crew, EUR 2 to 3 m). Run as coded, it gives EUR 2.33 m for one season and EUR 2.72 m for two (private use, Western Mediterranean, moderate use). Chapter 1's EUR 4 to 6 m is 12 to 15 percent of the purchase price of a new build costing roughly EUR 27 to 50 m, with 12 to 16 crew (ch08). The calculator FAQ and the ch01 FAQ now state these assumptions and the cause of the gap: crew numbers, and maintenance, insurance and berth costs that rise with value. The calculator FAQ also no longer gives 12 to 15 percent as a guide to its own output, which is about 7 percent of its own new-build value (EUR 34 m). Ch01's description of the calculator's sources is corrected: IGY is removed, and Christie Yachts and the TSR OpEx Survey are added. "Source assumptions are named on every line" is replaced in two places, because the lines carry no sources.

**Crew (item 6).** In the calculator, crew is 46 to 48 percent of the total, while its FAQs said 30 to 40 percent. Both FAQs now say so. The crew FAQ said 11 to 13 crew at 50 m; the model has 11, and 13 is its 55 m figure. It now separates base salaries (EUR 737,000) from total crew cost (EUR 1.0 to 1.4 m). It also explains that the salary page's EUR 1.5 to 2.4 m for 14 crew differs mainly in headcount and rotation; the same model at 14 crew gives EUR 1.39 to 1.63 m. The ch08 caption's "30 to 40 percent of the whole" is corrected to 37 to 45 percent. On the table's own lines, crew is 49 to 53 percent of the lines shown, which make up 75 to 85 percent of the budget. In ch07, "a third lighter" for sail becomes "a quarter", from ch08's 9 to 12 against 12 to 16.

**Premium (item 2).** The ch05 data-spread caption now names the three measures: contract price, brokerage purchase price at the same date, and later resale value. It also says that 30 to 50 percent compares the first two. Essay paragraphs 1 to 3 are untouched.

**Evidence (item 4).** In the calculator FAQ, "empirical band" becomes "practitioner ranges".

## Flagged (not edited)

- **Premium basis.** The archive's "comparable" brokerage yacht has no stated age. On Yatco's year-3 loss of 20 to 40 percent, a 30 to 50 percent premium fits a yacht about two to four years old. The author should confirm the basis.
- **Depreciation balance (editor approval).** Add to the "After delivery" row that the first-year loss on a yacht costing 30 to 50 percent more equals 13 to 30 percent of the brokerage price, so depreciation alone does not favour new build. Separately, "the premium does not amortise over a shorter hold" (ch05 paragraph 71, FAQ, checklist) implies that something recovers the premium, which the book never shows. This belongs to item 7.
- **Anecdotal fee range against the colophon.** Ch05 calls the 1 to 3 percent owner's representative range "anecdotal", but the colophon excludes anecdotal numbers. Option A applies if the range covers five or more projects: drop "anecdotal" and cite the archive. Option B is to revise the colophon so that it also covers labelled working ranges and illustrative models. A colophon text is in the register. Ch05's "5 to 15 percent" rise in build cost under first-draft contracts has no source.
- **Ch01 cost-composition source line.** It cites YPI, Quay, Pantaenius and MYBA, but none of them publishes percentage shares. These are practitioner allocations.
- **Other cost figures.** In ch01, USD 15 to 22 m over five years on a USD 30 m yacht is 10 to 14.7 percent a year, below the chapter's own 12 to 15 percent band. In ch08, the EUR 1.5 m budget on a 35 m motor yacht is 5 to 8 percent of its build price. In the calculator FAQ, "5-7 crew, EUR 300,000-450,000" at 30 m does not match the model (5 crew, EUR 390,000 to 530,000).
- **Crew numbers.** Ch08 and the salary page give up to 16 crew on a 50 m motor yacht; ch07's role list reaches 14, or 15 with a second engineer. The author should name the extra posts or narrow the range.
- **Salary page (editor approval).** Add base-salary arithmetic: EUR 0.87 to 1.27 m for a 14-post roster on the page's own bands. The page has no second-engineer band, so the ETO band is used as a proxy.
- **Ch02 Buyer B.** EUR 2.1 m on a 52 m is about 7.5 percent of a brokerage price. Chapter 1's percentages are of original capex, so the two do not compare directly. Case facts are unchanged.

## Not reconciled

- The ch06 "5 to 15 percent of insured hull value" for a five-year survey or major refit cannot be reconciled with the per-metre ranges without an insured value.
- The ch01 statement that crew is 30 to 40 percent of the budget sits below what ch08's own table implies (37 to 45 percent). The ch08 caption is corrected, but chapter 1's 30 to 40 percent, repeated in ch07, the ch07 case, the ch07 data spread and the salary page, should be checked against the archive. It may need to read 30 to 45 percent.

## Buyer journey (numbers only)

A reader moving from ch01 to the calculator met two incompatible 50 m figures with no explanation. The Ready edits close that gap. The calculator's TSR band check then places EUR 2.3 m in the EUR/USD 2 to 4 m band and ch01's EUR 4 to 6 m in the 4 to 6 m band, which is consistent.
