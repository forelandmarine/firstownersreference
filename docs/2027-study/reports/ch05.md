## Factual concerns and status

**C8, stage payments. Resolved, with a source question.**
- Para 2 said 20 to 30 percent at signature plus 50 to 70 across the build, which puts 70 to 100 percent before delivery. Para 26 said 50 to 70 percent before delivery. The EUR 12 million example: about EUR 9 million before launch, which is 9 / 12 = 75 percent. That is outside 50 to 70 and is reached before launch, not delivery.
- The source of the example is MacNally's TSR 230 article ("The unsecured owner", same paragraph). The article also says that in custom yacht work the pre-delivery share is "routinely well over half, and on some projects as much as 90 per cent (Port Royal Group)".
- Fix: para 2 now gives signature 20 to 30 percent, milestone instalments, and a balance at delivery, and points to the first contract point. Para 26 keeps 50 to 70 percent (Watson Farley & Williams) as the shipbuilding norm, then adds the custom yacht figure, up to 90 percent (MacNally, citing Port Royal Group). The EUR 9 million is now described as three quarters of the price. One set of figures is used throughout.
- The data spread's EUR 100 million schedule reaches EUR 90 million before delivery while its caption said 50 to 70 percent; the caption now describes the table, and the row "50–70 percent across build" follows para 2. The checklist and FAQ ("above 70 percent sits outside the industry-typical range") now carry the custom-yacht figure.
- **Flagged:** TSR 230 attributes "40 per cent or more" to Watson Farley & Williams for commercial shipbuilding. The essay, checklist, FAQ and data spread attribute "50 to 70 percent" to the same firm. I could not find the WFW source online. The author should confirm which figure WFW gives. I did not change the attribution.

**C24, refund guarantees. Resolved.** Para 11's sentence that buyers holding enforceable guarantees or registered title "generally kept their boat or their money" is the TSR 230 finding and stays as written. A sentence now follows it: in the TISG proceedings of 2026, a court suspended the guarantees as well, at least provisionally. This links it to the 6 July Florence order (paras 13 to 14). The TISG dates and facts were not changed.

**C26, case arithmetic. Resolved.** The commission is EUR 4.75 m, which is 5 percent of EUR 95 m. The variation margin is 40 percent × EUR 13 m = EUR 5.2 m. 4.75 / 5.2 = 0.913, so the commission was about 91 percent of the margin, not 8 percent. The case now reads "equal to about 90 percent". Other case figures check: 95 + 13 = 108; 2.6 − 1.2 = 1.4, which is 117 percent of 1.2.

**Para 36 against para 68 (structural read). Resolved.** The claim that "an afternoon's review saves more than the representative fee" was unquantified, and "It is consistently not done" had no source. The sentence now uses the chapter's own case: the change-order clause let the yard take EUR 5.2 m in variation margin, more than the 1 to 3 percent fee (EUR 0.95 to 2.85 m on EUR 95 m). The case takeaway and the data-spread caption are aligned.

**Para 74, paint. Corrected, author to confirm.** "Six- to nine-figure line item on a 50 to 80m hull" is impossible: nine figures is EUR 100 m or more. It now reads "six- or seven-figure". "Most common single category of warranty argument" (fairness flag 20) is now "a frequent subject".

**"Fail"/"failure" (author decision 5).** None of the essay, case, checklist, FAQ or data spread now uses either word about yards or builders. Para 73's "Comfort-class failure" was reworded. "Failure to notify" (para 31) is a legal term about the owner and stays. Q&A answers that cannot be edited still contain the word: Maltby ("if the yard fails to deliver", "if the yard fails") and Velema ("failure modes", about representatives). The author may want to ask Maltby whether he would accept "becomes insolvent" in his answers.
