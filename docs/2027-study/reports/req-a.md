## Depreciation (FOR-001 to 003)

The reviewer is right. IYC's "most yachts reaching 40-50% of their original price in five years" means value kept, a loss of 50 to 60 percent. This reverses the reading in the earlier research pass. Yatco publishes two different sets of figures:

- market article, 30 Oct 2025: 10 to 15 percent in year one, then about 5 percent a year, which comes to about 30 to 35 percent by year five if the 5 percent is of the purchase price
- depreciation guide, 27 Jun 2025: 10 to 20 percent in year one, 30 to 40 percent by year three, 40 to 50 percent by year five, all cumulative loss

The range at year five is therefore about 30 to 60 percent, not 30 to 50.

How the curve is now built: the table and chart show each source separately. Chart points are the midpoints of each published range. The IYC line runs straight from year 1 to year 5, and the chart caption says so. The band runs from the lowest to the highest published figure at years 1, 3 and 5. Years 7 and 10 are removed because no source gives a figure, and so is the synthetic "typical hull" line. The changes appear in the ch01 essay, data spread, chart 01.03, FAQ, the depreciation tool page (FAQ, table, hold cost, Dataset schema), the ch08 cross-reference and data spread row, and the calculator comment. The calculator's own model (57 percent retained at year five) falls inside the new range and is unchanged.

## Tariffs (FOR-013)

The text had already been rewritten. I checked it against the sources:

- The Supreme Court ruling was on 20 Feb 2026.
- Section 122 applied at 10 percent from 24 Feb to 24 Jul 2026.
- USTR announced the Section 301 final action on 23 Jul 2026. It took effect on 24 Jul and was published in the Federal Register on 28 Jul (91 FR 47318). For the EU it tops duty up to a combined 10 percent.

I added one point: yachts over 24 m carry a 1.5 percent MFN duty (HTS 8903.23 and 8903.33, checked on hts.usitc.gov), so an EU-built yacht pays 10 percent in all. The UK "flat 10 percent" is from the earlier research pass and is not rechecked here.

## MOA (FOR-024)

Under the MYBA MOA, the buyer gives notice of acceptance or rejection after the sea trial and condition survey and before completion. There is no 24 to 72 hour window after delivery. The 2021 E-MOA no longer fixes the deposit at 10 percent. I rewrote the ch04 closing paragraph and the ch05 data spread payment row to match. Outside this row: "Standard MOA terms allow 4 to 8 hours" for the sea trial (ch04) is not confirmed by any source checked.

## VAT (FOR-025)

Standard rates now apply only to supplies by VAT-registered businesses and to imports. The text also says that a private sale of a yacht on which EU VAT has already been paid does not normally attract VAT again.
