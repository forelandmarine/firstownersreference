## Factual concerns and status

**C1, 2025 market (para 0). Resolved, with two points flagged.** The opening sentence now names the measure and the source: "the total value of yacht sales above 24 metres reached USD 8.5 billion (Knight Frank Wealth Report 2026)". Secondary reporting of the Knight Frank report confirms "the total value of sales increased by 70 percent to $8.5 billion, an achievement second only to the post-pandemic exploit of 2021" (https://top-yachtdesign.com/report-yacht-market-sales/). That source does not state the 24 metre threshold or whether new-build sales are included. The author should check both against the Knight Frank report itself. "Eclipsed in the past decade only by" became "second in the past decade only to", which is the wording of the source. **For the ch02 pass:** ch02 15 ("strongest year on record by value") should say that it is Edmiston's brokerage figure for yachts over 30 metres. BOAT International's own brokerage figure for 2025 is reported as EUR 7.5 billion (https://www.boatingnz.co.nz/2026/02/global-super-yacht-market-in-sustained-value-boom-with-new-zealand-reflecting-the-shift/). Three different measures are now in circulation. The OBBBA attribution to BOAT International also appears only second-hand online. The data spread cites Stewart Campbell at the Global Order Book, which the author should confirm.

**C2, weeks of use (paras 12 and 13, FAQ 5). Resolved by naming the samples, with the sources flagged.**
- Ocean Independence: typical owners use the yacht for 4 to 8 weeks, and committed users for 12 to 16. I could not find this in Ocean Independence's published material. Its charter versus ownership article (https://www.oceanindependence.com/articles/charter-vs-ownership/) gives no usage figure. **The author should supply the source document.**
- Superyacht Report OpEx Survey, Q2 2026: owners on private programmes are aboard an average of 17 weeks. The respondents were captains and first officers of 28 to 83 metre yachts (https://www.superyachtnews.com/operations/opex-survey-for-captains-and-first-officers). The 17-week figure itself is not online and is presumably in TSR print. An older crew survey (SuperyachtNews, July 2015, more than 1,000 crew on 655 yachts) found owners "most likely" aboard for two to three months, which is consistent with the TSR figure (https://www.superyachtnews.com/owner/do_owners_spend_more_time_on_board_than_we_think). It is not added to the book.
- The ch09 case, 80 to 120 days, is one adviser's estimate for one buyer (about 11 to 17 weeks). It is not a sample.
- 12 weeks is the book's own threshold.
Para 13 now explains the gap as a difference of samples: "a brokerage house's figure for typical owners" against "a captain-reported survey of larger yachts in active private use". The FAQ now gives both figures and states the 12-week threshold. The old FAQ sentence "Above that, charter operation is the only economically rational frame" contradicted the essay and is removed.

**C3, depreciation (paras 16 to 18, FAQ 3). Recalculated. The source question is flagged.**
Working on the stated rates (10 to 20 percent in year one, then 6 to 8 percent a year compounding on the reduced value for years two to five):
- Low: 0.90 × 0.94⁴ = 0.90 × 0.781 = 0.703 retained, so 29.7 percent lost.
- High: 0.80 × 0.92⁴ = 0.80 × 0.716 = 0.573 retained, so 42.7 percent lost.
- Year-five loss: **about 30 to 43 percent**. Para 16 and the FAQ now say this.
- Para 18 follows from it: USD 30m × 0.297 to 0.427 = **USD 8.9 to 12.8m**, so "USD 9 to 13 million" (previously 12 to 15).

**Flag: the sources could support either figure.**
- If the 6 to 8 percent is read as a share of the *original* price (straight line), year five is 10 + 24 = 34 to 20 + 32 = 52 percent. The old "40 to 50" sits inside that range.
- IYC's guide (https://iyc.com/blog/yacht-depreciation/) gives 10 to 20 percent for year one and says "most yachts reaching 40-50% of their original price in five years". It gives no annual rate, and its wording could mean 40 to 50 percent lost or 50 to 60 percent lost.
- Other secondary sources give 5 to 8 or 5 to 10 percent a year after year one, without saying whether that compounds.
- I kept "compounding" because the text already said so. If the author's source means straight line, the alternative is "a further 6 to 8 percent of the purchase price each year", with year five at "roughly 35 to 50 percent" and para 18 at USD 10 to 16 million.

**Not changed, flagged:** the data spread table and the `depreciation-curve` chart in lib/charts.tsx show year five at 33 to 48 percent (year 2 at 16 to 28, year 3 at 22 to 35). These follow neither method exactly. On the compounding basis, years 2, 3 and 5 would be 15 to 26, 21 to 32 and 30 to 43. lib/charts.tsx is outside the files I may target, and editing the table alone would separate it from its chart. The author should decide, then change the table and the chart together.
