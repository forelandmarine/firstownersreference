**Carbon (para 18, spread, case). Resolved with working, and the source is flagged.**
- Royal Huisman's sustainability page (https://www.royalhuisman.com/sustainability) has no sail-versus-motor figure, and I found none elsewhere.
- The figure now rests on the chapter's fuel figures: IMO factor 3.206 t CO2 per tonne of marine gas oil × 0.86 kg/l = about 2.75 kg/l (DESNZ about 2.78).
- Motor: 111,000 to 269,000 litres × 2.75 = about 305 to 740 t a year. Sail: 33,000 to 92,000 litres × 2.75 = about 92 to 254 t.
- Matching the ends of each range: 33/111 = 0.30 and 92/269 = 0.34, so the sailing yacht emits 66 to 70 percent less. The midpoints give 67 percent. "60 to 70 percent" therefore becomes "about two thirds".
- The scope is now stated: fuel burned aboard only, tank-to-wake. It excludes well-to-tank emissions (about 0.63 kg/l more, per DESNZ), the build, refits, sails and travel. The comparison assumes the sailing yacht is sailed. "The gap is large, and durable." is cut.
- The case drops the Royal Huisman attribution; its 60 to 65 percent (52m against 48m) is close.
- **Flag:** the case's hybrid "35 to 45 percent" and para 22's "meaningful fuel reductions" are unsourced; published Heesen figures vary (45 to 80 l/h at 10 knots).

**C3, depreciation (para 50, spread). Resolved.**
- "Single-digit ... against 5 to 8 percent" contrasted two single-digit figures. It also contradicted ch01, where the typical hull loses 3 to 5 percent a year by year 7.
- Para 50 now applies ch01 17: the best builders on both sides lose single-digit percentages a year after year five. It keeps the broker-aggregated claim that quality sail holds value better, without a figure.

The seven-year figure is recomputed as capex plus opex minus residual. The inputs are:
- motor capex C between EUR 18m and 30m, with opex at EUR 1.5m a year;
- sail capex 0.65 to 0.80 C and sail opex 0.70 to 0.85 of the motor figure;
- the residual at year 7 is 45 to 60 percent, from ch01's spread.

| Residual assumption | Sail below motor on seven-year cost |
|---|---|
| Both yachts lose value at the same rate | 17 to 33 percent |
| Sail keeps 10 points more of its value | 25 to 41 percent |

So the old "25 to 40 percent" holds only with a residual advantage. The essay, checklist, FAQ and spread now say so.
