Most edits replace false physical metaphors ("sits", "flows", "lands", "travel with") and abstract substitutes ("structure", "economics", "architecture", "discipline", "framing") with the person, payment or action meant. Headings now say what each section contains ("The charter delusion" becomes "Does charter income cover the cost?"). Chapter-level points of logic:

- **ch01 essay, para 1.** "The demand structure underneath did not" now says what it appears to mean: a tax-driven rush to close does not by itself show wider or lasting demand. ARGUMENT, because it makes an implicit claim explicit; please confirm the meaning.
- **ch01 case, 28 percent sentence.** Rewritten plainly. Working: EUR 3.1 m / 0.28 = EUR 11.1 m, consistent with the EUR 11 m hull in the case. Total first 18 months: 3.1 + 4.0 + 0.8 = EUR 7.9 m, matching "approximately EUR 8 m".
- **ch02 essay, geographic claims.** The paragraph announced three claims but the section tests four (US, Europe, Asia, Russia). Corrected to four.
- **ch02 essay, Germany.** "Output concentrated at the very top" replaced with the arithmetic: 78,651 GT / 18 = 4,370 GT, "about 4,400 GT each".
- **ch02 case (composite).** Rewritten as an illustration: two 2025 transactions set on one yacht for comparison, stated in the standfirst, first paragraph, meta and disclosure. Removed the constructed details: the two buyers looking at the yacht in the same three-month window, "the other buyer was Buyer A", "six weeks later", and the quoted broker lines. Figures kept. Working: 27.8 / 26.5 = 1.049 and 27.8 / 25.0 = 1.112, so "5 to 11 percent above the adviser's estimate of fair value"; MYBA at EUR 27.8 m = 1.0 + 0.5 + (7.8 x 0.025) = EUR 1.695 m. The closing paragraph that restated the one before it was deleted. I removed the old claim that 14 months was well beyond the median, because N&J's Q3 2026 average for yachts that sold is 445 days, about 14.6 months.
- **ch02 checklist, hold price.** The item said "a hold price ... below which the buyer is comfortable walking away". The logic was inverted: a buyer walks away above a ceiling. Corrected.
- **ch02 data spread.** "This tracks the second consecutive contraction in order-book unit count" linked a brokerage price average to the new-build order book without evidence. Removed. "Wealth creation has accelerated" is not shown by a five-year change; replaced by the measured growth. The Swiss family office survey is no longer presented as "the buyer behind a first purchase", and its conclusion is qualified.
- **ch03 case.** "He held the yacht for another four years. The arithmetic compounded." Removed. The files are dated 2024, so four further years runs past 2026 (as with the ch05/ch06 fix in the brief), and recurring savings add up; they do not compound.
- **ch03 essay.** Sea Expandary was called "a larger ... counterparty" than Fincantieri. Fincantieri is the larger group, so "larger" was dropped. Roch's "authority ... did not" sentence is now attributed to him rather than stated as the book's finding. "The cheapest insurance available" (essay) and "cheapest form of structural insurance" (case) are replaced with claims the figures support.
- **ch03 data spread.** "Only a quarter ... handle a yacht in-house" corrected to the survey's measure, boat **or aircraft** management. The reason given for not naming firms now matches the essay ("the published record does not show which firms take them"), replacing "the practice is industry-wide".
- **Chapter title.** "How the industry actually works" becomes "How the industry works" in sections.json and lead-essays.json. The slug is unchanged; please check print running heads and any hard-coded title.
