## Buyer-journey review (item 15)

A first-time buyer can follow the book from cost (ch01) to market (ch02) to how advisers are paid (ch03) to buying (ch04) to build or buy (ch05), then to ownership (ch06 to ch08) and the hiring decision (ch09). Each chapter stands alone and the pointers between them work. The gaps and duplications:

- **Order of decisions.** The build-or-buy decision (ch05) comes after the brokerage acquisition process (ch04), and motor or sail (ch08) comes after operations. A buyer reading in order meets the process before the choices that precede it. The hiring order and ownership structure in ch09 are needed before anything in ch04; ch04 points to the adviser but not to the ch09 hiring order by name.
- **No closing synthesis.** ch09's ten questions test the advisers, not the purchase. Nothing gathers the decisions the book asks for (intended use, hold, budget, build or buy, motor or sail, structure, team) into one sequence before the closing argument.
- **Finance.** Yacht finance and lending appear only as a factor in structuring (ch04, ch09). A buyer who will borrow has no account of lender requirements, loan-to-value or how a lender interacts with stage payments.
- **Semi-custom.** Treated within ch05 as a timetable variant; the buyer choosing between a semi-custom hull in build and a brokerage yacht has little specific guidance.
- **Duplication.** The independence test appears in ch03 (summary), ch09 (full), the 09a page, the colophon and two FAQs; the hiring sequence in ch03 para 39 and ch09. These are pointers or summaries and are acceptable, but any future change to the test now has six places to update.

## Additional recommendations (report only, not implemented)

1. **Evidence-methodology note.** A short note (colophon or ch01 spread) saying how aggregated Foreland project data are selected: number of files, period, size band, and that figures are ranges across at least five projects, never one file. This answers the standard without exposing any client.
2. **Arrangements that worked.** ch03 already credits the experienced broker (paras 23 and 24). One documented example per chapter where a broker-led or management-led arrangement, properly disclosed, served the buyer well would make the disclosure standard read as practical rather than adversarial. Only from sources already held.
3. **A positive account of ownership.** ch01 "The case for ownership" and the ch08 case are the strongest material; a fuller account of what ownership gives (family time, crew continuity, programme control) would balance the cost chapters. The item 7 edits begin this by naming those reasons explicitly.
4. **Reading routes.** A short route on the contents page for three buyers: brokerage (01, 03, 04, 07, 09), semi-custom (01, 03, 05, 08, 09), fully custom (01, 02, 03, 05, 09, then 06 and 07). This also answers the order-of-decisions point above without restructuring.
