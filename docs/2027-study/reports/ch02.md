## Overall assessment

The chapter's figures are good and mostly well sourced: Knight Frank, the Global Order Book, Edmiston and Northrop & Johnson. The problems were the claims built on top of them. Three sentences drew conclusions the figures do not support: "serious inventory to move quickly", "overwhelmingly American, increasingly self-made", and "inventory rots for a year and a half". The tariff paragraph was out of date by two changes of legal basis. The closing run (34 to 40) went from London property to a hotel simile. The edits keep every figure and recast the claims around them. The essay falls from about 2,340 to 2,170 words, even though the tariff paragraph is longer.

**C21, tariffs (para 24). Corrected with sources. The author should confirm the EU ceiling.** The "fifteen percentage points" matched the IEEPA-era EU rate. It no longer applies. The sequence:
- On 20 February 2026 the Supreme Court held in Learning Resources v. Trump that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs (https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/us-supreme-court-holds-ieepa-does-not-authorize-tariffs).
- On 4 March 2026 the Court of International Trade ordered refunds to all importers of record (https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/court-of-international-trade-orders-nationwide-tariff-refunds; https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/03/Court-of-International-Trade-Orders-Refunds-for-IEEPA-Tariffs).
- A temporary 10 percent tariff applied under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 from 24 February 2026, for 150 days (https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/02/trump-temp-global-tariff-after-scotus-ieepa-ruling).
- Section 122 lapsed on 24 July 2026. From that date USTR imposed Section 301 duties of 10 or 12.5 percent on 60 economies (https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1603-ustr-takes-final-action-imposing-tariffs-on-60-trading-partners-as-section-122-tariffs-expire; https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/us-administration-rebuilds-global-tariff-program-under-section-301).
- For EU goods the combined MFN and Section 301 duty is capped. Morgan Lewis and Ibec report the cap as 10 percent. A PwC note reads it as 12.5 percent.

The paragraph now gives the dated sequence and says there is "a ceiling on the combined duty for goods from the European Union", without stating the cap. It advises the buyer to confirm the rate at contract and delivery and to allocate any change in the contract. The BoatPro IEEPA rate list stays as history. The dealer-inventory point is now dated to 2025.
- **Flag:** please check the EU cap against the USTR fact sheet or the Federal Register notice, and whether the rules on yachts imported for foreign-flag use change the picture. If you prefer a figure, "10 percent" is the majority reading.
- The IMF paragraph (25) is cut. Its projections were dated, and its link to "the tariff regime" no longer holds.
