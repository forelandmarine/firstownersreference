## Factual concerns and status

**C15, overrun cause against contingency. Resolved.** Para 18 blamed the 30 to 50 percent overrun on poor specification and weak project management. Para 19 said it was "what the work actually costs ... not a project management failure". Maltby's answer settles it: the overrun is "partly a pricing problem and partly a contract problem".
- Para 18 keeps the consensus figure and Dockwalk; para 19 splits the overrun into hidden work (budgeted for) and uncontrolled specification and management (contained); a new paragraph shows the 10 to 25 percent reserves cover a well-managed refit (10 to 20 percent over) but not 30 to 50.
- Working, on the case's EUR 3.2 m quote: 20% × 3.2 = EUR 0.64 m reserve; 40% × 3.2 = EUR 1.28 m overrun; 1.28 − 0.64 = EUR 0.64 m unfunded.
- Aligned: data-spread standfirst and overrun table, FAQ 1, checklist contingency (10 to 25 percent). The case blockquote attributed to the essay is deleted.

Para 10 shows the arithmetic; USD 5 to 10 m full scope sits at the top of the band and above. The archive is named as Foreland's. Spread heading, columns, rows and caption corrected.

**C17, slot lead times. Resolved.** Para 66 had top European yards booked 12 to 24 months ahead; para 67, the checklist and FAQ 3 said book 12 to 18 months ahead, too late for those yards. All now say book as far ahead as the chosen yard's order book: up to 24 months at top European yards, less in the US or for smaller scope.

**C10, insurance cycle. Resolved within ch06** by removing paras 74 and 75 (see Repetition). ch07 settles the dates.
