## 1. Summary: top 10 changes by importance

1. **The war risk section is stale, and the 2026 Iran war is missing.** US and Israeli strikes on Iran began on 28 February 2026. Hormuz has been effectively closed for most of the year. Iran resumed attacks on shipping on 8 July 2026. The Houthis have blockaded Bab al-Mandeb since 21 July 2026. Al Jazeera, citing market data on 23 July 2026, puts Hormuz war risk at 7.5 to 10 percent of hull value and Bab al-Mandeb at 0.5 percent. Black Sea rates went back above 1 percent in July 2026 (The Insurer). The book's "current" Black Sea figures (0.65 to 0.80, 0.45 to 0.55) are a December 2025 snapshot. Recommendation: print war risk as a dated snapshot with the mechanism explained, never as "current". Add the 2026 conflict to the notice-of-cancellation paragraph.

2. **The insurance outlook ("2026 reads as stable") needs a 2027 frame.** Published position for 2026: hull rates are flat to single-digit reductions for clean yacht risks, capacity is at multi-year highs, and wind capacity is returning to South Florida (Insurance Journal, March 2026; WTW Q1 2026). P&I general increases for 2026/27 were 5 percent at Shipowners' Club, which also raised deductibles 10 percent and named fires, US claims and crew quality as yacht claim drivers. Other clubs: 7.5 percent at UK P&I, and a reported 8 percent at Steamship Mutual. Gallagher Specialty (8 September 2026) forecasts 2.5 to 5 percent general increases for the 20 February 2027 renewal. Club announcements normally land in November and December, probably after print.

3. **The Bayesian paragraphs are overtaken.** Italian prosecutors' preliminary findings (May 2026) put the cause in how the yacht was operated, not the weather. That contradicts the MAIB interim report (May 2025). The MAIB final report is still pending with no date. The builder's group entered court-supervised crisis proceedings in July 2026, and a EUR 456 million civil claim with counterclaims is live. The book's contingency, "if findings land on builder or designer", should become an open question. Under the house rule, name no individuals.

4. **Quay Crew is misquoted in several places (A).** The 2025/26 report (published 17 November 2025, 367 captains) records 70 to 79m pay up 7 percent against the 2023 survey. That is a two-year movement, not "year on year". The 63 percent on time-for-time rotation covers all captains surveyed, not the 70 to 79m bracket. The wrong versions appear in ch01 para 9, the ch07 data spread caption and table, ch07 para 13, the ch07 FAQ, the salary tool and the Erica Lay question.

5. **Captain pay bands no longer match the cited YPI guide (A).** YPI's 2026 captain guide (9 March 2026) gives 30 to 40m EUR 6,000 to 9,000, 40 to 50m EUR 8,500 to 12,500, and 60 to 80m EUR 14,000 to 18,000. It calls 100m+ "discretionary". The ch07 data spread and the salary tool show higher bands attributed to YPI. Either re-align them or attribute the difference to Foreland data (at least five projects per band).

6. **Add the MLC 2006 2025 amendments (in force 23 December 2027).** They cover shore leave without a visa, defined repatriation costs (including luggage and medical treatment), violence and harassment (including bullying), key worker status, fair treatment and medical guidance. They bite on charter yachts under MLC, so SMS, DMLC and crew agreement updates will run through 2027. Also add the 2022 amendments in force since 23 December 2024: reasonable-cost internet access, free food and drinking water, and properly sized PPE.

7. **The charter regime list is partly out of date today (A), before 2027.** Greece has allowed non-EU flagged yachts over 35m to charter under the e-Charter Permission since February 2024 (up to 28 days a year). Spain has replaced the charter licence with an Affidavit of Responsibility (Royal Decrees 186/2023 and 587/2024), with 2026 as the transition year. The Monaco 90-day claim was directly challenged in The Superyacht Report on 16 June 2026 ("The Monaco flag myth"). The Cayman code name is dated: Cayman applies the REG Yacht Code, July 2024 edition. Malta now has its own Commercial Yacht Code (CYC 2025).

8. **Fuel and FX assumptions need a 2027 check.** Brent passed USD 100 on 8 March 2026, peaked near USD 126, and was about USD 91 in mid-August. Rotterdam LSMGO was about USD 1,271 per tonne on 18 August 2026. Ch01's "Mediterranean diesel at EUR 1.30 to 1.80 per litre" has no named source, and marina pump prices reported for 2026 sit higher. The calculator converts at EUR/USD 1.09. The ECB-area rate was about 1.16 on 11 September 2026.

9. **Crew mobility and certification are changing for the 2027 season.**
   - The EU Entry/Exit System has been fully live since 10 April 2026. Non-Schengen crew get no stamp to prove their days, and port handling has been inconsistent.
   - ETIAS slipped out of 2026. The new timeline is expected from the eu-LISA board in September 2026, with a 2027 launch and at least a six-month grace period.
   - The MCA launched the Yacht Unlimited CoC on 18 May 2026 (MSN 1858 Amendment 2). It widens the pool of masters and chief mates for yachts above 3,000 GT, and an engineer route is due in 2026.

10. **Lithium-ion and environmental rules reach operating budgets.**
   - MCA MGN 681 (M) Amendment 1 (19 December 2025) sets storage and charging standards for e-toys. Secondary sources say certified containers are required from 1 January 2027 (verify against the MGN text). Insurers are tying underwriting to it (IUMI, June 2026).
   - The North-East Atlantic ECA enters into force on 1 September 2027. The 0.10 percent sulphur limit applies from 1 September 2028. NOx Tier III applies to yachts of 24m and over constructed on or after 1 January 2027.
   - The Mediterranean SOx ECA has been in force since 1 May 2025.

Also: every "2026" headline, FAQ question and "current to" line in the three tools needs reframing, or a decision on whether to keep the 2026 slugs.

### 3.1 MLC 2006: 2025 amendments in force 23 December 2027 (add to ch07 compliance section and FAQ `07[1]`)

The amendments were adopted by the fifth Special Tripartite Committee (April 2025), approved at the International Labour Conference on 6 June 2025, and expected to enter into force on 23 December 2027. They cover:
- shore leave without a visa or special permit, irrespective of flag
- repatriation costs spelled out (passage, accommodation en route, up to 30 kg luggage, medical treatment)
- violence and harassment, including bullying, from recruitment to complaints
- seafarers as key workers
- fair treatment after casualties or detention
- medical guidance aligned to the International Medical Guide

Relevance: charter yachts certified under MLC. Flag states will issue implementing notices during 2027, and managers will need to update crew agreements, DMLC Part II and complaint procedures.

The 2022 amendments are already in force since 23 December 2024: reasonable-cost internet access, free food and drinking water, and PPE in suitable sizes.

Draft: "Further amendments to the Maritime Labour Convention are expected to enter into force on 23 December 2027. They strengthen shore leave, define what repatriation must cover, and require measures against violence and harassment on board. For a charter yacht this means revised crew agreements and complaint procedures during 2027, normally handled by the management company. Amendments in force since December 2024 already require reasonable-cost internet access, free food and drinking water, and protective equipment in the right sizes."
