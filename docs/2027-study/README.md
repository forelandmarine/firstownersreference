# Forward to 2027: pre-print study

Prepared 13 September 2026 for the editors. Position at that date unless stated.

The 1st Edition was written through 2026 as a 2026 book. Print has moved past the Monaco window (the marketing plan had files at the printer on 21 August), so first readers will hold the book from late 2026 and through most of 2027. This study asks two questions of every dated line in the book and the site: is it still right, and will it still be right for a reader in 2027.

Method. All 457 sentences in the content JSON that carry a year or a time word were indexed (`dated-sentences-index.tsv`). Five research passes then read the chapters in full, checked each claim against current published sources, and built the 2027 picture for their chapters. The full reports, with every source URL, sit alongside this file:

- `00-structure-and-edition.md`: edition naming, imprint, ISSN, year-stamped URLs, print pipeline hazards
- `A-ch01-ch07-costs-crew-insurance.md`
- `B-ch02-market.md`
- `C-ch03-ch05-industry-yards.md`
- `D-ch04-ch09-legal-tax.md`
- `E-ch06-ch08-refit-environment.md`

Verification caveat. The research passes ran out of search budget before every lead was closed. Items marked unverified in the reports must not print as settled. Several important facts rest on one secondary source and need a primary check before they go in: the ECB rate rise of 10 September 2026, the BOATPro H1 2026 brokerage total of EUR 4.05 bn, the Captain Arctic sail count, the ETIAS timetable, and the MGN 681 container date.

## 1. Decisions for the editors first

These decide how much of the rest applies.

1. **Press close date.** Several things resolve between October and December 2026: the Camper & Nicholsons long stop (30 September), the Italian Sea Group signing (about 26 October), P&I general increases for the February 2027 renewal (November to December), the IMO Net-Zero Framework vote (4 December), the BOAT Global Order Book 2027 (December, on pattern), and the MarineMax and Denison closings (by year end). Recommendation: close copy in mid-December 2026 and print in January 2027, so the book carries outcomes rather than pending events. The cost is no finished copies for the Superyacht Forum in November; proofs can serve there.
2. **Edition year.** Recommendation: badge it "1st Edition, 2027", with one book-wide "Position at [close date]" stamp in the imprint. If it prints in January 2027 the copyright line becomes 2027. The imprint's "next edition September 2027" then becomes the 2028 edition. Settle this before the ISSN application goes in, so the registration and the cover agree.
3. **The 2026 Iran war in print.** Two research passes found it independently (from 28 February 2026; Hormuz effectively closed for much of the year; Bab al-Mandeb blockade from 21 July). It touches ch02 macro, ch07 war risk and cruising grounds, and ch07 insurance. Recommendation: a dated, factual backdrop line in each place, no commentary.
4. **Paid data.** Several current figures (H1 2026 brokerage, 2026 US share, APAC and Russian ownership) are only available through BOATPro or SuperYacht Times State of Yachting 2026. License, or print what is free and say so.
5. **Tool URLs.** `/tools/yacht-vat-2026`, `/tools/captain-and-crew-salary-2026` and `/tools/yacht-insurance-cost-2026` carry the year. Search equity is negligible (salary page 452 impressions, no clicks; the other two absent from Search Console). Recommendation: yearless slugs with permanent redirects, the year in titles and H1s only. Year-in-query demand is real ("global order book 2026", "yacht refit 2026"), so the title keeps it.

## 2. House rules to adopt for the 2027 pass

1. Every figure that moves carries a "Position at" date. One date for the whole book, set at copy close.
2. Yard build slots are given as lead time from contract, never as calendar years ("three to five years from contract", not "a 2030 Lürssen").
3. No relative time words in lead essays or data spreads: "recent", "currently", "this year", "now", "over the past five years", "at this writing". Name the year.
4. Guest Q&As stay as given. Where events have moved since the interview, add a dated editors' note beneath.
5. Where law or regulation is unsettled, print it as an open point for counsel, not as a forecast.
6. Data sources are named by edition ("BOAT International Global Order Book, 2026 edition"), so a later edition does not silently falsify the text.

## 3. Wrong today

Errors that stand whatever the edition year. Fix in one pass before the forward work.

### 01 The reality of ownership, 07 Operations, cost tools
- Quay Crew misquoted in about six places (ch01 p9, ch07 p13, ch07 data spread, ch07 FAQ, salary tool, Lay question): the 7 percent rise in the 70 to 79 m bracket is against the 2023 survey, not year on year; 63 percent on rotation is all 367 captains, not that bracket.
- Captain pay bands credited to YPI 2026 in the ch07 data spread and salary tool do not match YPI 2026 (30 to 40 m EUR 6,000 to 9,000; 40 to 50 m EUR 8,500 to 12,500; 60 to 80 m EUR 14,000 to 18,000). Re-align, or state the Foreland variance openly.
- Greece: non-EU flags over 35 m can charter under the e-Charter Permission since February 2024, up to 28 days a year.
- Spain: the charter licence is replaced by an Affidavit of Responsibility (Royal Decrees 186/2023, 587/2024). Matriculation tax is 12 percent (11 in the Canaries), not 4.
- Cayman applies the REG Yacht Code (July 2024 edition), not a "Cayman Large Yacht Code". The calculator still cites the MCA LY3 code, replaced in 2019.
- P&I limit written as EUR 500 million in the ch07 FAQ and checklist; it is USD.
- Calculator converts at EUR/USD 1.09; about 1.16 at 11 September 2026.
- 50 m crew cost: EUR 1.5 to 2.4 million (salary tool) against EUR 900,000 to 1.4 million (calculator).
- US passenger carriage between US ports is the Passenger Vessel Services Act, not the Jones Act (verify).
- Unsourced under the pricing rule: Mediterranean diesel EUR 1.30 to 1.80 per litre; compliance cost EUR 80,000 to 200,000 on a 40 m; calculator P&I 0.3 to 0.4 percent and charter uplift 30 to 40 percent; Gallagher Q4 2025 "4 to 7.5 percent" (source not found).

### 02 Reading the market
- Tariff paragraph (p23) describes a regime that has ended. The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026; a 10 percent Section 122 surcharge ran to 24 July 2026; a Section 301 regime and the EU-US 15 percent ceiling apply since. Customs counsel to sign off the rewrite.
- Macro paragraph (p24) cites the IMF October 2025 outlook as "moderating". IMF July 2026 update: 3.0 percent global growth for 2026, 3.4 for 2027, inflation 4.7 percent.
- Order book table, 2025 column: 40.0 m and 507 GT on BOAT's own figures, not 39.6 m and 529 GT. Changes are up 0.8 m and up 8.7 percent.
- DreAMBoat was delivered 45 months after contract, about 18 months faster than a conventional build of its size. Not "in 18 months" (ch02 p10, FAQ, tracker).
- Unsourced: asking prices "softened around 7 percent from 2023 peaks" (also in the ch02 case); the Denison days-on-market series (cited URL dead); "BOAT EUR 7.5 bn over 30 m" (Edmiston publishes EUR 7.1 bn).
- Ch01 opening: "the market grew because the tax code changed" overstates Knight Frank, which also names pricing adjustment and equity markets.

### 03 How the industry actually works
- Ya Mon is presented as dismissed and closed. It has been revived in amended form and is open (Eleventh Circuit opinion 10 June 2026). Check docket 1:24-cv-20805.
- MarineMax agreed on 10 August 2026 to be acquired by Safe Harbor Marinas (Blackstone Infrastructure) for USD 1.5 bn. Fraser and Northrop & Johnson will not sit inside a listed MarineMax.
- OneWater agreed on 9 September 2026 to sell a majority of Denison to OceanWorld Group.
- C&N "structural and permanent" conflict: control is changing hands. Stake 80 percent against 81.4 percent in one report; settle from the Lai Sun shareholders' agreement.
- "The roll-up is essentially complete" and "minority by deal volume": four major brokerages changed or agreed to change ownership between November 2025 and September 2026; no source for the volume measure.

### 04 The acquisition process, 09 The decision framework, VAT page, glossary
- Malta leasing "5.4 to 6.12 percent over 24 metres" in four places (ch04 essay and spread, ch06, ch07). The table was replaced by actual-use guidelines of 12 March 2020; the Commission closed its case on 30 October 2020.
- "Italian leasing largely curtailed" (ch04, ch07). Italy moved to an effective-use test from 1 November 2020; leasing remains available on documented use.
- Glossary Temporary Admission still requires a third-country stamp to reset. The EC Guidance Note (30 April 2026) sets no minimum time outside; ADM Circular 11/2026 accepts proof of reaching international waters. Add the statutory 10-year total.
- VAT page post-Brexit section ties EU status to flag and says the UK and EU separated "post-2024". They separated on 1 January 2021.
- Glossary OBBBA entry gives a 2029 end date. Relief is permanent (IRS Notice 2026-11), as ch07 already says.
- French Commercial Exemption: "six conditions", five listed.
- Phi is 58.5 m, not 60 (BOAT, SuperyachtNews).
- Red Ensign Category 2 cap: 400 GT (FAQ) against 150 GT extendable to 400 (glossary).
- ch09 FAQ gives the YORR name and founding date inconsistently with ch05.

### 05 New build versus brokerage
- Two FAQs say the "Yacht Owners' Register of Representatives" was founded in June 2023. The register is the Yacht Owner's Representative Register, launched November 2024; June 2023 was the YORP announcement, first course July 2023.
- YORR threshold is projects over 30 m, not 40.
- "Present order-book expansion" contradicts ch02's record of a second year of contraction.
- Project Solent: Cecil Wright "is acting", not "acted". Delivery summer 2027.
- Standfirst "24 to 36 month commitment" conflicts with 36 to 60 months for custom builds in the essay.
- Italian Sea Group paragraph stops at April 2026. Protective measures partly revoked 10 June; court procedure filed 1 July (about EUR 400 m debt); investor sale with binding offers 15 October and signing about 26 October 2026.

### 06 Refit
- Rybovich: Safe Harbor is owned by Blackstone Infrastructure since 30 April 2025, not Sun Communities.
- Amico & Co is part of Amico Marine (May 2026), CEO Bruno Guglielmini.
- MB92 sites are Barcelona, La Ciotat, Vallauris Golfe-Juan and Neom. "Compositeworks and Monaco Marine" attribution unsupported.
- FAQ places Amico (Genoa) in Northern Europe and Lürssen refit in Bremen; it is Hamburg.
- "In 2026, a sellers' market for the first time in a decade": no public source found.

### 08 Motor versus sail
- EU ETS scope in four places: it applies to ships of 5,000 GT and above carrying passengers or cargo for commercial purposes. A private yacht is outside at any size. Charter yachts of 12 passengers or fewer are unsettled.
- ETS cost EUR 200,000 to 400,000 sits on a 90 m in the essay and on 110 m plus in the table; no source.
- FAQ phase-in wrong: 100 percent intra-EU coverage has applied since 2024. What rose is the share surrendered: 40, 70, then 100 percent from 2026 emissions, first due 30 September 2027.
- Sail share of the order book is about 6 percent (69 of 1,093), not 12.
- Methanol and hydrogen "at concept and pilot stage": already in delivered or launched hulls (Sanlorenzo 2024, Feadship Breakthrough, Lürssen 114 m launched 2025).
- Captain Arctic: two rigid solar sails, 36 passengers, delivery late 2026 (check with Pelorus, since the guest source said five).
- Internal: crew counts (6 to 9 against 9 to 14, or 9 to 12 against 12 to 16); seven-year cost formula; 580 kWh attributed to both AERA and Aquarius II.
