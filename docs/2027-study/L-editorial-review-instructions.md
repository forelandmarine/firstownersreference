## Binding rules
- Restrained British English; do not make the writing promotional, conversational or simpler at the cost of precision. Do not rewrite passages that already work.
- **Client confidentiality is non-negotiable.** Foreland cases are anonymised under NDAs. Never request, infer or add identifying details. Examples may stay general.
- **Chapter 05 paragraphs 1 to 3 are author-approved.** Do not edit. Flag factual concerns separately. (Paragraph 3 was corrected with author approval on 14 Nov: the gap now "usually widens during construction".)
- Do not invent figures, citations, project details or credentials. Do not introduce new commercial claims.
- Implement verified factual and grammatical corrections. Flag disputed figures and interpretations.

- "Additional recommendations" are reported separately, never implemented.

## Priority 1
1. Reconcile annual operating cost figures: ch01 (about EUR 4 to 6m a year, 50m new-build motor), running-cost calculator FAQ (EUR 2.3 to 2.8m, 50m motor, moderate use), ch08, FAQs. Compare age, build price, value, crew, region, season, use. Keep legitimate differences but make the assumptions explicit and consistent so a reader sees why they differ.
2. Ch05 new-build vs brokerage premium: essay and data spread must explain the same relationship. Distinguish contract price, brokerage market value and resale value. Check the 30 to 50 percent premium's comparison basis. Do not imply depreciation alone favours new build. (Essay paras 1 to 3 protected; see above.)
3. Publisher's independence disclosure: ch09 has six criteria, the colophon five answers. The professional indemnity answer was removed on the author's instruction earlier ("not relevant on this publication"). Do NOT add an insurance answer or limits; prepare the mapping and flag for the author. Verify SYBAss/YORR wording: correct forms are "SYBAss accredited" and "YORR registered" (Foreland is registered on the Yacht Owner's Representative Register via YORP completion; YORR is operated jointly by SYBAss, IAMI and the Superyacht Alliance).
4. Evidence standard: the colophon says pricing comes from published market evidence or aggregated practitioner data covering at least five projects. Ch05 calls some owner's representative fee figures anecdotal practitioner ranges. Classify figures as published benchmark, aggregated project data, or illustrative model; qualify unsupported precision; apply consistently. Either the figures meet the stated standard or the text says what they are (or the colophon describes the standard accurately).
5. Ch09 independence and suitability tests: allow for NDAs (anonymised project histories, references supplied with client consent, redacted evidence). Refusal to disclose protected client information is not evidence of lack of independence. Keep verifiable competence and transparent commercial relationships.
6. Reconcile 50m crew numbers and crew costs across the calculator, ch01, ch07, ch08 and salary reference pages. Distinguish base salary from total employment cost; check percentage allocations against modelled totals; explain differences by type, programme, rotation.

## Priority 2
7. Qualify ownership thresholds (twelve weeks of use; seven to ten years to amortise a new-build premium) as practitioner guidelines, not universal tests; separate financial efficiency from privacy, convenience, continuity, control and preference.
8. Review categorical conclusions (always, never, must, only; typically, generally, normally; best, safest, most appropriate; guarantees, prevents, eliminates). Check support and qualification; keep well-supported ones.
9. Remove repetition of: broker commissions and referral arrangements; the owner's representative's commercial role; adviser independence; early engagement of specialist counsel; written fee disclosure. Keep the strongest explanation in its home chapter; elsewhere a short pointer.
10. Dense technical passages, chiefly ch03 and ch05: separate facts from implications for the buyer; fewer organisations, cases and percentages per sentence; better punctuation and transitions; keep terms and detail.
11. Natural English: no abstract nouns doing unnatural things, mechanically assembled sentences, formulaic contrasts, forced metaphors, rhetorical questions, promotional conclusions.
