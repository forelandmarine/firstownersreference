## 1. Summary (top 10)

1. **EU ETS scope is misdescribed in four places.** The Directive and the MRV Regulation cover ships of 5,000 GT and above *transporting passengers or cargo for commercial purposes*. A privately used yacht is outside at any size. Only a 5,000 GT+ yacht on commercial charter can fall inside, and the position of yachts certified for 12 passengers or fewer is unsettled. The lead essay (ch08 p13), data-spread caption, FAQ ch08[2] and checklist ch08 group 2 all present GT as the only test. Section 3 has replacement wording.
2. **Chapter 08 contradicts itself on who pays ETS.** The lead essay puts EUR 200,000 to 400,000 a year on "a 90m". The data spread table says a 90m is "borderline" and puts that cost band on "110m+". The EUR figure has no source. Lürssen's 114m methanol fuel-cell yacht (Project COSMOS, launched August 2025) is 6,300 GT, which is a usable data point. Recommend deleting the EUR band or sourcing it, and correcting the GT table.
3. **FAQ ch08[2] gets the phase-in wrong.** It says "From 2026 the system covers 100 percent of intra-EU emissions and 50 percent of voyages from a non-EU port". The 100/50 geographic coverage has applied since 2024. What steps up is the share of emissions surrendered: 40 percent (2024 emissions), 70 percent (2025), 100 percent (2026 onward, first surrendered by 30 September 2027). The lead essay and data-spread caption have this right.
4. **The IMO section stops at the 2023 Strategy.** It needs the Net-Zero Framework: approved at MEPC 83 (April 2025), adoption adjourned by vote in October 2025, unchanged through MEPC 84 (April to May 2026). The resumed extraordinary session to vote is on **4 December 2026**, straight after MEPC 85 (30 November to 3 December 2026). Earliest entry into force is 2028. This should print as an open question.
5. **The book has no NOx Tier III or emission control area content, and 2027 is when this matters most.** The **North-East Atlantic ECA** was adopted 1 May 2026 (MEPC.407(84)). It covers UK, Ireland, Atlantic France, Spain and Portugal, Iceland, Faroes and Greenland, enters into force 1 September 2027, and takes 0.10% sulphur from 1 September 2028. Tier III NOx applies to ships with a building contract on or after **1 January 2027** (or keel from 1 July 2027, or delivery from 1 January 2031). A buyer signing a new build in 2027 who wants to cruise Atlantic Europe is in scope. The Canadian Arctic and Norwegian Sea ECAs entered into force 1 March 2026, with 0.10% sulphur from **1 March 2027**. The Mediterranean has had a SOx ECA since 1 May 2025, and a Mediterranean NOx ECA is still at study stage (REMPEC Focal Points, about May 2027).
6. **The methanol and hydrogen sentence is already wrong in 2026.** Ch08 p17 says hydrogen fuel cells are "at concept and pilot stage". Delivered or launched hulls include:
   - Feadship Breakthrough (118.8m, hydrogen fuel cell)
   - Lürssen's 114m methanol fuel-cell yacht (launched 2025)
   - Sanlorenzo Almax/Contigo (50m, methanol reformer fuel cell, delivered May 2024)

   Due by 2027 are Sanlorenzo LIFE MYSTIC (green methanol bi-fuel) and an e1 Marine 0.5 MW methanol-to-hydrogen installation.
7. **Captain Arctic details are out of date.** September 2026 reporting describes **two** rigid aluminium solar sails with about 20,000 sq ft (roughly 1,860 m2) of panels, not five 35m sails. The vessel is a 36-passenger, 2,200 GT expedition ship, floated out in August 2026, with delivery "late 2026". It is a passenger ship, not a yacht, which matters for Norway's fjord rules.
8. **Refit yard facts need correction for 2027.**
   - Rybovich's owner Safe Harbor was sold by Sun Communities to **Blackstone Infrastructure** (closed 30 April 2025).
   - Amico & Co now sits under **Amico Marine** (May 2026), CEO **Bruno Guglielmini**, not Manuel Di Tillio.
   - MB92 sites are Barcelona, La Ciotat, Vallauris (Golfe-Juan) and Neom. It has a **EUR 40 million+ Barcelona programme 2026 to 2028** (dry-dock renewal, quays, new 680 t Travelift) and a concession to 2050. The "Compositeworks and Monaco Marine" attribution is not supported.
   - FAQ ch06[2] puts Amico (Genoa) in Northern Europe, and Lürssen refit in Bremen when it is Hamburg.
   - Palumbo Superyachts (seven Mediterranean yards) is missing.
9. **Environmental rules that land in 2026 and 2027 and are not in the book:**
   - Polar Code chapters 9-1 and 11-1 now apply to pleasure yachts of 300 GT and above. Existing yachts must comply by the first intermediate or renewal survey after 1 January 2027.
   - The cybutryne antifouling ban requires removal or sealing no later than 60 months after the last application, so coatings applied in 2022 must be dealt with by end 2027. This is a refit scope item.
   - Norway's World Heritage fjords are zero-emission for passenger ships under 10,000 GT from 2026, and for larger ships from 2032.
   - UK ETS covers 5,000 GT+ domestic voyages from 1 July 2026, and offshore from 1 January 2027.
   - Australia's new biofouling arrival rules for non-commercial vessels apply from 17 June 2026.
   - IMO is drafting a legally binding biofouling instrument, target about 2029.
   - FuelEU Maritime is not mentioned anywhere.

## 3. Corrections where the 2026 text is already wrong

### 3.1 EU ETS scope (ch08 p13, data-spread caption and table, FAQ 08[2], checklist 08 g2 i0)

**What the law says.** The MRV Regulation (EU) 2015/757 Art. 2 and ETS Directive Annex I apply to ships of 5,000 GT and above for voyages "for the purpose of transporting passengers or cargo for commercial purposes". Coverage is:
- 100 percent of emissions on voyages between EEA ports and at berth
- 50 percent on voyages into or out of the EEA

Surrender is phased: 40 percent of 2024 emissions (by 30 September 2025), 70 percent of 2025 emissions (2026), and 100 percent from 2026 emissions (first full surrender by 30 September 2027). Methane and nitrous oxide are included from 2026. Offshore ships of 5,000 GT and above join from 2027.

The Commission FAQ does not mention yachts. Trade and legal press (SuperyachtNews, 8 January 2026; BOOTE) report two things. First, the Commission has indicated yachts of 5,000 GT+ carrying passengers for commercial purposes are in scope. Second, yachts that cap guests at 12 to avoid passenger-ship classification "typically sit outside", with "commercial purpose" undefined.

**Review.** On 17 July 2026 the Commission proposed an ETS revision:
- Selected categories of 400 to 5,000 GT would come in from 2031; passenger and ro-pax ships of that size are left out, with a feasibility report due December 2031.
- MRV would extend to passenger ships of 400 to 5,000 GT from 2029.
- Ice-class derogations run to end 2035.
- The EU aims for agreement in Q1 or H1 2027.

Yachts are not named.

**Suggested replacement, lead essay ch08 p13:**
> EU ETS Maritime has applied since 1 January 2024 to ships of 5,000 GT and above that carry passengers or cargo for commercial purposes to or from EEA ports. It counts all emissions on voyages between EEA ports and at berth, and half the emissions on voyages into or out of the EEA. Companies surrendered allowances for 40 percent of 2024 emissions and 70 percent of 2025 emissions. From 2026 emissions onward the figure is 100 percent, first due by 30 September 2027. Methane and nitrous oxide were added from 2026. A yacht in private use sits outside the system at any size. A yacht of 5,000 GT or more on commercial charter can fall inside it, and published guidance does not settle the position of charter yachts certified for 12 passengers or fewer. Very few yachts reach the threshold: Lürssen's 114 metre fuel-cell yacht launched in 2025 measures 6,300 GT, while a 60 metre motor yacht is typically 700 to 1,200 GT. In July 2026 the European Commission proposed extending the system to selected ship types between 400 and 5,000 GT from 2031, leaving passenger ships out for now. The proposal does not name yachts, and negotiation between Parliament and Council runs through 2027.

(Check the 700 to 1,200 GT figure against the book's own source. It is carried over, not re-verified.)

**Suggested replacement, FAQ ch08[2]:**
> Only in limited cases. EU ETS Maritime applies to ships of 5,000 GT and above carrying passengers or cargo for commercial purposes. A privately used yacht is outside the system at any size, and most yachts are far below 5,000 GT in any case. A very large yacht on commercial charter can fall inside it. The share of emissions paid for rose from 40 percent (2024) and 70 percent (2025) to 100 percent from 2026 emissions onward. Methane and nitrous oxide were added in 2026. A Commission proposal of July 2026 would bring some ship types of 400 to 5,000 GT into the system from 2031; passenger ships and yachts are not among them. HVO (hydrotreated vegetable oil) is a drop-in diesel replacement with a lifecycle CO2 reduction of up to 90 percent, where certified waste-derived supply is available.

**Suggested replacement, checklist ch08 g2 i0:**
> EU ETS Maritime exposure. The system applies only to ships of 5,000 GT and above used for commercial passenger or cargo transport. Confirm the yacht's GT, its commercial or private status, and its passenger certification with the flag and the yacht lawyer.
> detail: A privately used yacht is outside at any size. A July 2026 Commission proposal to extend the system below 5,000 GT does not name yachts; watch the 2027 negotiation.

**Data spread table.** Change the "EU ETS exposure" column for the 90m and 110m+ rows to "Only if 5,000 GT+ and on commercial charter". Remove the "EUR 200–400 k" figure unless sourced. For a sense of scale: at the late-August 2026 EUA price of about EUR 82 per tonne, EUR 200,000 to 400,000 implies roughly 2,400 to 4,900 tonnes of in-scope CO2. That is plausible for a large yacht but not documented. Take the EUA price at press from ICE Endex or EEX, not from the secondary tracker used here. The caption's phase-in wording is correct; add "for commercial purposes".

The essay/table conflict (90m vs 110m+) disappears once the commercial test is added and the EUR band is removed.
