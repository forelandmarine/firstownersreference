## 1. Summary (top 10)

1. **The chapter is written as a 2026 snapshot throughout and will read as stale from January 2027.** The title, standfirst, the h2 "Reading 2026 correctly", "this year", "a first-time buyer entering in 2026" (paras 11, 17, 31), the FAQ questions ("in 2026") and the SEO title are all 2026-bound. Recommended title: **"Reading the market"**, with a dated position stamp. A second choice is "Reading the market into 2027". Section 6 sets out a structure that does not date within six months.

2. **The macro paragraph (para 24) is now contradicted.** It cites the IMF October 2025 WEO and says "the macro backdrop is moderating". Since then:
   - The US and Israel began a war with Iran on 28 February 2026, with disruption in the Strait of Hormuz.
   - The IMF July 2026 Update cut 2026 global growth to 3.0 percent, put 2027 at 3.4 percent and raised 2026 global headline inflation to 4.7 percent.
   - The ECB **raised** its deposit rate to 2.50 percent on 10 September 2026.
   - The Fed held at 3.50 to 3.75 percent on 29 July 2026 by 9 votes to 3, with all three dissenters voting to hike. Its next decision is 16 September.

   The paragraph must be rewritten, and needs a position stamp.

3. **The tariff paragraph (para 23) describes a regime that has ended.**
   - The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 February 2026 (Learning Resources v. Trump, 6 to 3).
   - On 4 March 2026 the Court of International Trade ordered nationwide refunds.
   - A 10 percent Section 122 surcharge ran from 24 February to 24 July 2026.
   - Since 24 July a two-tier Section 301 regime has applied (10 or 12.5 percent), while EU goods sit under the EU-US framework's 15 percent all-inclusive ceiling.

   The "fifteen percentage points" point for Northern European hulls broadly survives, but now rests on the EU-US deal, not on IEEPA. The paragraph needs rewriting and a stamp. Editors should confirm it with customs counsel.

4. **US 100 percent bonus depreciation is permanent.** IRS Notice 2026-11 (14 January 2026) confirms permanent 100 percent first-year depreciation for property acquired after 19 January 2025, with no phase-down. The book's framing holds for 2027. Some broker sites claim a 2029 end date; that is wrong on the IRS text. Keep the point, and cite the IRS notice in ch07.

5. **The order book figures will be superseded in December 2026.**
   - The 2027 BOAT Global Order Book counts contracts as at 1 September 2026. On the last two cycles it was presented in December (the 2026 edition live on 11 December 2025) and printed in the January issue.
   - BOAT's own 2026 report forecasts the order book will "settle between 950 and 1,000 superyachts in build annually" over the next few years. This is the single most useful published forward marker for the chapter, and it is not in the book.

6. **Two order book figures in the data spread and the tracker are wrong against BOAT's own reports.** The 2025 edition was 1,138 yachts, 45,487 m and 577,482 GT. That is an average of about 40.0 m and 507 GT, and BOAT's 2026 text says length rose "just 0.8m" and GT rose "8.7 per cent from last year's 507 GT". The book prints 39.6 m, "up 1.2 m", 529 GT and "up 22 GT". Separately, ch08 para 2 puts sail at "roughly 12 percent of units on order", but the order book gives 69 of 1,093, about 6 percent (ch02 and the tracker say six).

7. **Brokerage momentum carried through 2026.**
   - BOAT reported sales at a three-year high in H1 2026. A secondary write-up of BOATPro data gives 281 sales worth EUR 4.05 bn in asking value, against EUR 3.61 bn in H1 2025.
   - July 2026 came in at EUR 762.4 m across 52 sales, up 89.3 percent (BOAT, 10 August 2026).
   - Northrop & Johnson's H1 count differs: 326 sold (down 8 percent), USD 3.51 bn (up 15 percent), and 2,157 yachts for sale worth USD 18.8 bn as at 1 July 2026.

   The data spread's H1 note ("above EUR 3.35 bn, a floor") should be replaced with a named full figure. Aggregates should not be printed without their definitions.

8. **The new-build pipeline is thinning and splitting, which matters for 2027 and 2028.**
   - Northrop & Johnson counts 936 yachts in build at 1 July 2026. Scheduled deliveries are 466 in 2026, 301 in 2027, 121 in 2028 and 39 in 2029, and it calls the later years a floor.
   - H1 2026 new-build contracts fell to 142 from 177.
   - Ferretti recorded **zero** superyacht orders in H1 2026 and cited the Middle East.
   - Sanlorenzo's order intake rose 18.3 percent. About 40 percent of its 2027 volume and about 25 percent of its 2028 volume is already sold.
   - The Italian Sea Group entered insolvency protection in 2026. The consortium bid excludes restarting existing superyacht builds.

   This is the evidence base for a 2027 slot and yard-risk section.

9. **Most wealth sources have newer editions than the book cites.**
   - Capgemini World Wealth Report 2026 (4 June 2026): HNWI wealth USD 98.3 trn, 25.3 m HNWIs.
   - Altrata World Ultra Wealth Report 2026 (23 June 2026): 556,850 UHNW individuals, forecast 746,570 by 2030.
   - UBS Global Wealth Report 2026 (30 June 2026).
   - UBS Global Family Office Report 2026 (28 May 2026): 307 family offices, average net worth USD 2.7 bn. A full-text search of the 2026 PDF finds no mention of yachts or boats, so the ch09 claim can be extended to "2025 and 2026 editions".

   Knight Frank's 2026 figures, used for the chapter's core claims, are the latest available. Its 2027 edition is expected in spring 2027, after print.

10. **Several statements need correction or a source before print:**
   - Para 10 and the data spread say Oceanco "delivered the 111m DreAMBoat in 18 months". Oceanco's release says the contract was signed in February 2022 and delivery was in November 2025. The yard cut about 18 months off a typical schedule; the build did not take 18 months.
   - "Asking prices have softened around 7 percent from 2023 peaks" (para 19, and repeated in the case study) has no named source.
   - The Denison figures (277 days, 573 days, reductions 1.36 to 0.76, US-built 130 against 103): the cited URL now redirects to a listings page with no report, and Denison's March 2026 report does not carry these figures.
   - "BOAT International reports around EUR 7.5 billion over-30 metre" could not be verified. Edmiston's 2025 review gives EUR 7.1 bn and 363 yachts for the 30 m+ market.
