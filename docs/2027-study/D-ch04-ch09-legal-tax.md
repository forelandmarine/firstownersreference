## 1. Summary (top 10)

1. **Malta's leasing figures are out of date in four places.** Ch04 lead essay, ch04 data spread, ch06 and ch07 print "5.4 to 6.12 percent on yachts over 24 metres". The length-based table was replaced by Commissioner for Revenue guidelines of 12 March 2020, based on actual use. The Commission closed the infringement on 30 October 2020. The book's own VAT page says "reformed", so the book currently contradicts itself. Update all four.
2. **"Italian leasing has been largely curtailed" is inaccurate.** It appears in ch04 (essay and spread, "rarely advisable in 2026") and ch07. Italy replaced flat-rate presumptions with an effective-use test from 1 November 2020, and added user declarations from 2021. Leasing is still available on documented use. Update.
3. **2027 AML rules are the largest gap in ch04 and ch09.** Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (AMLR) applies from 10 July 2027. Three articles bear on a first owner:
   - Article 74: traders must report every non-commercial sale of watercraft at or above EUR 7,500,000 to the FIU, and banks handling the transaction must report too.
   - Article 67: a non-EU company or trust acquiring such a yacht from a trader must first file its beneficial owners with an EU central register.
   - Article 80: an EU-wide EUR 10,000 cash limit.

   Neither chapter mentions any of this. Add a dated box to ch04 and an editor's note after the Quadrant interview in ch09.
4. **The glossary "Temporary Admission" entry is now wrong.** It says TA "resets when the yacht leaves EU waters and obtains a third-country customs stamp". The 2026 position is different. The EC Guidance Note sets no minimum time outside. ADM Circular 11/2026 accepts proof of reaching international waters. The glossary contradicts the VAT page. Update, and state the statutory 10-year cap (Article 251(4) UCC).
5. **The VAT page's post-Brexit FAQ and section need rewriting.**
   - It frames loss of EU status by flag ("UK-flag yachts ... lost EU status"). The page itself, and the EC note, say flag is not relevant.
   - It says UK yachts "lost EU Temporary Admission unless owner and users are non-EU established". That garbles the eligibility test.
   - It says "Post-2024 the UK and EU operate as separate customs territories". They have been separate since 1 January 2021.
6. **The glossary "OBBBA bonus depreciation" entry is wrong on dates.** It says "in force January 2025" and "placed in commercial service between 20 January 2025 and 21 December 2029". Ch07 correctly says the relief is permanent for property acquired after 19 January 2025. IRS Notice 2026-11 confirms 100 percent bonus depreciation is permanent. Fix the glossary to match ch07.
7. **The US tariff paragraph in ch02 is stale.** It says the Supreme Court "upheld" the lower courts after "argument in November 2025", with "roughly fifteen percentage points of tariff exposure". The Supreme Court decided Learning Resources v Trump on 20 February 2026 (6-3). Replacement 10 percent tariffs under Section 122 ran from 24 February to 24 July 2026. The Court of International Trade struck them down for three plaintiffs on 7 May 2026, and the ruling is under appeal. Section 301 investigations are under way to replace them. Reframe as an open point for 2027. Flag to the ch02 agent.
8. **Spain, Greece and ETS text in ch07/ch08 is stale or wrong.**
   - Spain (ch07): "4 percent matriculation tax" contradicts the glossary's 12 percent. The IEDMT rate is 12 percent, 11 percent in the Canary Islands.
   - Greece (ch07): "non-EU flags cannot charter Greek-to-Greek" has been superseded by the e-Charter Permission. Non-EU commercial yachts over 35 metres with non-wooden hulls may charter up to 28 days a year.
   - EU ETS (ch08, VAT page, glossary): describes scope loosely. The EC FAQ ties scope to ships carrying passengers or cargo for commercial purposes. Offshore ships of 5,000 GT and above come in from 2027. The Commission's ETS review proposal of 17 July 2026 would extend scope to certain ships of 400 to 5,000 GT from 2031.
9. **The VAT page should become a 2027-current reference, not a 2026 snapshot.** The two 2026 texts (EC Guidance Note of 30 April 2026, ADM Circular 11/2026) remain the operative guidance into 2027. The ADM Circular claims on the page were verified: Form 71-01, discharge via international waters, inward processing time excluded, charter exit and re-entry. Add a "What changes in 2027 and 2028" section and fix the errors above (see section 4).
10. **Ch09's independence test (element 6) and the Quadrant interview predate the transparency changes of 2025 to 2027.**
    - Element 6 talks of "jurisdictions without beneficial-ownership disclosure". Most registers are now not public but are legitimate-interest access. EU AMLD6 Article 74 had a transposition deadline of 10 July 2025.
    - Jersey and Guernsey consulted in early 2026. The Isle of Man consulted from 5 May to 30 June 2026.
    - The February 2026 EU non-cooperative list puts Anguilla and the Turks and Caicos Islands (both Red Ensign Group Category 2 registries) in Annex I, and the BVI in Annex II.

    Reframe element 6 and add a short dated transparency paragraph to ch09.

## 4. 2027 developments not yet covered, with suggested wording

All wording follows house rules: British English, no em dashes, figures past nine, currency codes, informational register. Where law is unsettled the wording says so.

### 4a. Ch04: new box after "VAT, flag, and registration" (replaces editor's note p[29])

> **What applies in 2027.** The customs position for non-EU yachts was set out in two 2026 texts that remain the working guidance: the European Commission Guidance Note for Pleasure Craft dated 30 April 2026, and Italian customs circular ADM 11/2026 of 15 May 2026. Neither changes the law. Together they confirm that flag and owner nationality do not decide a yacht's customs status, that Temporary Admission runs for 18 months per period with no minimum time outside the EU between periods, and that total time under Temporary Admission may not exceed 10 years. In Italy, proof of reaching international waters is enough to end a period; other Member States may ask for more.
>
> From 10 July 2027 the EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation applies. A trader selling a yacht of EUR 7.5 million or more for private use must report the sale to the national Financial Intelligence Unit, and banks handling the payment must report it too. A company or trust set up outside the EU that buys such a yacht from a trader must first file its beneficial owners with the central register of the Member State concerned. Cash payments above EUR 10,000 are prohibited. How these rules apply to brokered sales between private parties is not yet settled and is a point for counsel.

### 4b. Ch04 p[23] and data spread rows: Malta and Italy

> The Maltese leasing arrangement no longer uses a length-based table. Under guidelines issued by the Commissioner for Revenue on 12 March 2020, VAT on the lease follows the yacht's documented use inside and outside EU waters, estimated at the start and revised against actual use. The European Commission closed its infringement case against Malta on 30 October 2020. The effective rate depends on where the yacht goes and the evidence kept.

> Italy replaced its length-based presumptions with an effective-use test from 1 November 2020, and since 2021 the user declares actual use in EU waters. Leasing remains available on documented use.

Data spread row 3 condition: "Actual use inside and outside EU waters, evidenced by logs, AIS or GPS data; lease to a non-taxable lessee." Row 4 effect: "Available on documented effective use since November 2020." Delete "Rarely advisable in 2026".

### 4c. Ch09: editor's note after the Quadrant interview

> **Editors' note, September 2026.** Three changes due in 2027 bear on the structures discussed above. From 10 July 2027, a company or trust established outside the EU that buys a yacht of EUR 7.5 million or more for private use from a trader must file its beneficial owners with an EU central register before completion, and the sale is reported to the national Financial Intelligence Unit. From 30 September 2027, EU tax authorities begin exchanging data on residents' crypto-asset transactions for 2026. In the Crown Dependencies, the Isle of Man, Jersey and Guernsey consulted in 2026 on giving access to their beneficial ownership registers to people with a legitimate interest in preventing money laundering. Temporary Admission remains subject to a total limit of 10 years for each yacht. None of this changes the sequence Quadrant sets out; it adds filings to the timetable.

### 4d. Ch09 independence test, element 6

> 6. Are the firm's principals named, traceable, and accountable? If no, none of the above can be verified. Anonymous firms, firms that cannot show their beneficial owners are filed with a central register, and firms whose principals do not appear in public communications fail the most basic accountability test.

### 4e. Ch09: short paragraph for UK-resident owners (after p[12])

> Residence now matters more than domicile for owners with a UK connection. Since 6 April 2025 the United Kingdom has taxed by residence. Newcomers after 10 years abroad can claim relief on foreign income and gains for their first four years. Non-UK assets, including a yacht held through an offshore company or trust, fall within inheritance tax once the individual has been UK resident for 10 of the previous 20 tax years, and can remain within it for between three and 10 years after leaving. An existing structure set up under the old rules is worth reviewing with UK tax counsel.

### 4f. Ch09: jurisdiction list status (caption under ownership models table)

> The EU reviews its list of non-cooperative tax jurisdictions each February and October. At the February 2026 revision, Annex I included Anguilla and the Turks and Caicos Islands, and Annex II included the British Virgin Islands. Listing can bring defensive tax measures in EU Member States, so the status of a holding jurisdiction is checked at the time of structuring.

### 4g. Glossary: Temporary Admission (definition)

> Temporary Admission (TA) lets a yacht registered outside the EU, used by a person established outside the EU, stay in the EU customs territory for up to 18 months per period without paying import duty or VAT. The customs territory includes territorial waters. There is no minimum time outside the EU before a new period starts, but total time under TA may not exceed 10 years (Article 251(4) Union Customs Code). Commercial charter under a private TA period is not permitted, and a sale inside the EU ends the relief. Italy's customs agency accepts proof of reaching international waters, such as AIS data, log entries or foreign bunker receipts, to end a period; practice in other Member States may differ. Sources: European Commission Guidance Note for Pleasure Craft, 30 April 2026; ADM Circular 11/2026.

### 4h. Glossary: OBBBA bonus depreciation

> The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed in July 2025, made 100 percent bonus depreciation permanent for qualifying business property acquired and placed in service after 19 January 2025. IRS Notice 2026-11 confirms there is no phase-down. For a yacht, the relief depends on genuine business use, usually a documented charter operation, and on the passive-activity and luxury-use limits in the US tax code. The treatment of yachts used mainly outside the United States is a point for US tax counsel.

(Confirm the signature date, 4 July 2025, before print.)

### 4i. Ch07 p[58], p[59]: Spain and Greece

> Spain. Spanish residents and entities established in Spain register yachts over 8 metres locally and pay the special tax on certain means of transport at 12 percent of value (11 percent in the Canary Islands). Yachts used exclusively for charter can be exempt, subject to conditions that restrict use by owners, shareholders and their relatives. Foreign-flagged yachts owned by non-residents without a Spanish establishment are not required to register, although regular charter activity in Spain can be treated as creating an establishment.

> Greece. Since the e-Charter Permission system opened, commercially registered yachts under non-EU flags, over 35 metres and with non-wooden hulls, may charter in Greek waters for up to 28 days a year, subject to a special charter fee and VAT registration. EU-flagged commercial yachts use the three-year Greek charter licence. The TEPAI cruising tax applies to recreational craft over 7 metres regardless of flag.

### 4j. Ch02 p[23]: US tariffs (for the ch02 agent)

> The Supreme Court held on 20 February 2026, in Learning Resources v Trump, that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs. A replacement 10 percent tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 applied from 24 February to 24 July 2026. The Court of International Trade ruled against it for three importers on 7 May 2026, and the government appealed. Section 301 investigations were opened to follow it. The tariff on a European-built hull imported into the United States in 2027 depends on their outcome, which was not settled when this edition went to press.

### 4k. EU ETS (VAT page section 8, ch08, glossary)

> EU ETS Maritime applies to ships of 5,000 GT and above that carry passengers or cargo for commercial purposes, and from 2027 to offshore ships of that size. Since 2026 it covers 100 percent of emissions on voyages between EU ports and 50 percent on voyages to or from a non-EU port, including methane and nitrous oxide. Private yachts are not named in the Commission's guidance. On 17 July 2026 the Commission proposed extending the system to certain ships between 400 and 5,000 GT from 2031, with agreement targeted in early 2027. Whether large charter yachts would fall within scope is not settled.

### 4l. VAT page, post-Brexit section

> Since 1 January 2021 the United Kingdom and the EU have been separate customs territories. A yacht registered in the UK and used by a person established outside the EU, including a UK resident, can cruise EU waters under Temporary Admission on the same terms as any other non-EU yacht: 18 months per period and 10 years in total. Whether a particular yacht kept or lost EU Union status at the end of the transition period depends on where it was at that date and on the evidence held, not on its flag. Owners with a yacht that moved between the UK and EU around 2020 should confirm its status with counsel.
