# F. Structural read of the lead essays (Pass One)

Scope: content/lead-essays.json (chapters 01 to 09, about 27,300 words), read against sections.json, cases.json, checklists.json and guest-opinions.json. Paragraph indices are zero-based and refer to the `paragraphs` array of each essay (ch05 21 means chapter 05, paragraph 21). Case, checklist and Q&A references are labelled as such. No content was edited.

One note on the source: ch02 13 was changed in the JSON after the plain-text dump was made. It now reads "The number of potential buyers is rising sharply. The number of new yachts being contracted is falling." The order-book versus availability point is therefore resolved in that blockquote. Every other paragraph matches the dump.

---

## 1. Summary

The argument runs in a clear arc. Chapter 01 sets out what ownership costs. Chapter 02 describes the market the buyer is entering. Chapter 03 explains who is paid by whom. Chapters 04 to 08 apply that to the acquisition, the new build, the refit, operations and the choice of yacht type. Chapter 09 gives the team and the tests. Each chapter adds something real: cost ranges (01), supply and pricing signals (02), the commission and retrocession mechanics (03), process and VAT/flag (04), yard insolvency and the new-build contract (05), refit contracting and specification (06), insurance and charter regimes (07), carbon and the sail case (08), and the independence and suitability tests (09).

The problem is not missing argument. The same argument is opened again in several chapters, and reference material has piled up inside the essays. The conflict thesis from ch03 is argued again at length in ch05 59 to 68 and ch09 0 to 8. The insurance market is described twice (ch06 73 to 76, ch07 24 to 51). Builder's risk appears almost word for word in ch06 76 and ch07 48. The charter case studies appear in ch01 21 and ch07 67. The French Commercial Exemption and the Maltese and Italian leasing rules are set out in ch04 23, ch06 72 and ch07 56, 57 and 64. The Capgemini inheritance figures and the "two profiles" of reader appear in ch02 5 and ch09 4 to 5. About 2,500 words of directory and regulatory listing (ch06 29 to 64, ch07 55 to 66, ch04 26 to 31) sit inside essays that otherwise argue a case.

The five most important structural changes:

1. **Give the conflict argument one home, in ch03.** Define "independent adviser" and "owner's representative" there too; at present the role is only defined in ch05 39 to 45, though ch03 39 and ch04 0 to 4 already depend on it. Later chapters should apply the argument to their own situation, with a one-sentence pointer back, rather than argue it again.
2. **Settle the independence standard.** ch09 17 says a party paid on completion "fail[s] this test". ch03 25, ch05 62 and the ch09 case (para 9, "Pass with disclosure") treat disclosure as the remedy, and ch09 43 puts a commission-paid insurance broker on the recommended team. The book needs one stated rule: which roles must pass all six elements, and which need written disclosure only.
3. **Consolidate duplicated reference blocks** into one chapter each, leaving pointers elsewhere: insurance and Bayesian in ch07, builder's risk in ch06, charter economics in ch01, VAT regimes in ch04, inheritance demographics in ch02. Move the yard directory (ch06 29 to 64) and the charter-regime list (ch07 55 to 66) to data spreads or web-only blocks.
4. **Add the owner's lived experience and the positive case**, using material the book already holds. ch01 24 to 26 states the case for ownership in three paragraphs. The evidence of well-run ownership sits in the cases (ch01 case 15 to 16, ch07 case 10 to 12, ch08 case 14), the Q&As (Lay, Johnson) and one figure in the ch09 case (para 15, 40 to 60 days a year of the owner's attention). The essays draw on none of it.
5. **Fix the figures that disagree** (section 4) before the line edit, because several are reused in cases and checklists. The ones that matter most are commission arithmetic, depreciation, weeks of use, stage payments, Spanish IPR and the insurance cycle.

## 4. Contradictions and inconsistencies

**C1. 2025 as a record year.** ch01 0: 2025 "eclipsed in the past decade only by the post-Covid surge of 2021". ch02 15: "The brokerage market in 2025 was the strongest year on record by value." The measures differ (total market over 24m against Edmiston's brokerage over 30m), but read side by side they contradict each other. Name the measure in each.

**C2. Weeks of use.** ch01 12: "typical use at 4 to 8 weeks". ch01 13: "Owners are aboard an average of 17 weeks". The ch09 case (para 15): "80 to 120 days a year of the buyer's direct family use". ch01 26 sets 12 weeks as the threshold. One sentence in ch01 explaining the difference in sample would settle it.

**C3. Depreciation.** ch01 16 gives 10 to 20 percent in year one, then 6 to 8 percent a year compounding to year five, and says "By year five a typical hull has lost 40 to 50 percent". On those rates the year-five loss is about 30 to 43 percent. Separately, ch01 17 puts Feadship and Lürssen among builders losing "only single-digit percentages annually after year five", while ch08 50 contrasts sailing builders' "single-digit annual depreciation" with "5 to 8 percent for top motor builders". 5 to 8 percent is itself single-digit.

**C4. Commission arithmetic.** ch03 3 says a EUR 28 million sale "moves between EUR 1.4 million and EUR 2.8 million". Under the MYBA scale set out in ch03 4, EUR 28 million gives about EUR 1.7 million. ch03 4 says the buyer's broker typically captures "4 to 5 percent of gross sale price ... from either base", but on the MYBA scale the whole commission on USD 50 million is 4.5 percent. The ch02 case (para 5) has EUR 1.4 million on EUR 27.8 million, with a buyer-broker share of EUR 600,000 (about 2.2 percent).

**C5. Who pays.** The ch03 standfirst says "the buyer routinely pays for the seller's adviser". ch03 4 says the commission is "paid by the seller".

**C6. The independence standard.** ch09 17: parties earning on completion "all fail this test. They may still provide value, but not independence." Against that: ch03 25 ("Disclosure and clearly named roles solve most of it"); ch05 62 (YORR allows a disclosed tender commission); the ch09 case (para 9: insurance broker "Pass with disclosure"); and ch09 43 (an insurance broker paid by commission is on the recommended team).

**C7. Hiring order.** ch03 30: the captain is "the most consequential hire an owner makes, and often the first". ch09 37 to 41 and 45 put the captain fourth. ch09 10 says structure comes first, but the hiring order (37 to 43) has no structuring adviser.

**C8. Stage payments.** ch05 2: "20 to 30 percent at signature, 50 to 70 across the build, balance at delivery" (70 to 100 percent before delivery). ch05 26: "50 to 70 percent of contract value paid before delivery". The EUR 12 million example in ch05 26 (about EUR 9 million at risk before launch) fits para 2, not para 26.

**C9. Spanish IPR.** ch04 23: IPR "suspends 21 percent VAT on refit works ... within an 18-month admission window". ch06 70 to 71: time under inward processing "does not count against the temporary admission period", and Spanish law "exempts the works". The Maltby Q&A question text (editorial, so it can be changed) also says "suspends".

**C10. Insurance cycle.** ch06 75: increases "across 2022 to 2023 ... had already stabilised by H1 2024". ch07 26: "The hardened market of 2022". ch07 27: Davis "places the peak of the recent cycle in 2024".

**C11. Insurance brokers named.** ch07 32: Howden and Pantaenius are "the two largest", with PIB "worth a third quote". ch07 79 and ch09 43: "Pantaenius, AON, or Gallagher". The ch07 checklist: "at least three of Pantaenius, AON, and Gallagher Specialty". ch07 37 relies on Howden and Pantaenius templates.

**C12. Compliance cost.** ch01 10: regulatory and class compliance is 2 to 3 percent of operating cost. ch04 30: EUR 50,000 to 150,000 a year for "tax, structuring, and flag administration" on 40m. ch07 23: EUR 80,000 to 200,000 a year for "ISM, MLC, flag administration, class society fees" on 40m. Flag administration is counted in both, and ch07 23 applies ISM and MLC costs to a 40m yacht that ch07 20 says is usually outside both.

**C13. Fuel.** ch01 11: a 50m yacht "runs past EUR 250,000". ch08 5: a 50m motor yacht at 400 hours burns "EUR 200,000 to 350,000".

**C14. Fixed cost against use bought.** ch01 12: "a fixed cost structure that runs whether the yacht is used or not". ch08 49: "A EUR 1.5 million annual operating budget delivers 16 to 20 weeks ... against 12 to 14 weeks". The ch08 case (para 5) makes the same move.

**C15. Refit overrun.** ch06 18: overruns are driven by "poor specification and weak project management". ch06 19: the overrun is "what the work actually costs ... not a project management failure". ch06 18 also sets contingency at 10 to 25 percent against a "routine" 30 to 50 percent overrun.

**C16. Refit units.** ch06 8: "Working ranges per metre per year". ch06 9 lists mid-life and major structural refit per metre, and ch06 10 turns those into a project total (EUR 2 to 5 million on 50m).

**C17. Refit slot lead time.** ch06 66: top European yards are "booked 12 to 24 months ahead". ch06 67: "slot booking begins 12 to 18 months ahead".
