Decisions first

These five decide how much of the rest applies. Each carries a recommendation.

  1. Press close date

    Six things resolve between October and December 2026: the Camper & Nicholsons long stop (30 September), the Italian Sea Group signing (about 26 October), P&I increases for the February renewal, the IMO Net-Zero vote (4 December), the BOAT Global Order Book 2027 (December), and the MarineMax and Denison closings.

    RecommendClose copy mid-December, print January 2027. The book then carries outcomes, not pending events. Proofs can serve at the Superyacht Forum in November.

  2. Edition year

    The imprint already promises “next edition September 2027”. A book badged 2026 reads as last year’s by March.

    Recommend“1st Edition, 2027”, one book-wide position date in the imprint, copyright 2027 if printed in January. The next becomes the 2028 edition. Settle before the ISSN application.

  3. The 2026 Iran war in print

    Found independently by two research passes. It touches ch02 macro, ch07 war risk and cruising grounds, and the ch07 insurance outlook.

    RecommendA dated, factual backdrop line in each place. No commentary.

  4. Paid data

    H1 2026 brokerage, the 2026 US share and updated APAC and Russian ownership figures sit behind BOATPro or SuperYacht Times State of Yachting 2026.

    RecommendLicense BOATPro for the close window, or print what is free and say so on the page.

  5. Tool URLs carrying 2026

    Salary, VAT and insurance pages. Search equity is small: the salary page has 452 impressions and no clicks; the other two do not appear in Search Console. Searchers do type the year (“global order book 2026”).

    RecommendYearless slugs with permanent redirects. The year lives in the title and H1, so each annual update changes a title, not a URL.

House rules for the pass

So the book does not date again within six months of press.

  1. Every figure that moves carries a “Position at” date. One date for the whole book, set at copy close.
  2. Yard build slots are lead time from contract, never calendar years. “Three to five years from contract”, not “a 2030 Lürssen”.
  3. No relative time words in essays or data spreads: recent, currently, this year, now, over the past five years. Name the year.
  4. Guest Q&As stay as given. Where events have moved, a dated editors’ note sits beneath.
  5. Unsettled law prints as an open point for counsel, not a forecast.
  6. Sources are named by edition, so a later edition cannot silently falsify the text.

Wrong today

These stand whatever the edition year, so fix them in one pass before the forward work. Error means the text was wrong when written or is contradicted by the book itself. Moved means events have overtaken it. No source means it fails the pricing rule.

Backdrop without a single date

The 2026 Iran war and its effect on war risk: in July 2026 merchant shipping paid 7.5 to 10 percent of hull value for Hormuz, about 0.5 percent for Bab al-Mandeb, and above 1 percent in the Black Sea. US bonus depreciation is now permanent. The new-build pipeline is thinning: Northrop & Johnson counts 301 scheduled deliveries for 2027 and 121 for 2028 at 1 July 2026 (a floor), H1 2026 contracts fell to 142 from 177, and Ferretti took no superyacht orders in H1 while Sanlorenzo’s intake rose 18 percent.
