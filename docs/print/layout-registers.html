The First Owner's Reference  ·  1st Edition  ·  Print art direction

Layout registers

Sixteen page architectures for the 1st Edition. Every page in the book is one of these and nothing else. The registers exist to solve one problem: the publication carries no advertising, so the relief that a trade magazine buys from its advertisers has to be made editorially instead.

Measured against the comparison set. The August proof runs 120 pages at 230 × 300 mm. Fifteen of those pages carry fewer than 45 words, which is the working definition of a relief page: an opener, a full-bleed image, a closing device. That is 12 per cent of the block. The Superyacht Report 222 runs the same 120 pages at 210 × 265 mm and carries 30 relief pages, 25 per cent, most of them full-page advertising. The Knight Frank Wealth Report 2025 runs 88 pages at 215 × 275 mm with 10 relief pages, 11 per cent, and no advertising at all.

Per square millimetre of page the three books are typographically identical: roughly eight words per thousand square millimetres. The Reference is not set too small and it is not set too tight. What it lacks is rhythm. Between the chapter 05 opener and the chapter 06 opener the reader crosses fourteen consecutive pages without a single moment of relief; between chapters 06 and 07 it is sixteen; between the last chapter close and the colophon it is eighteen. No book in the comparison set asks that of a reader.

The rule that follows. No run of more than four consecutive full-text pages anywhere in the block, and no page above 850 words. Twenty pages currently break the second rule and the worst carries 1,276.

What this costs and what it buys. Applying the registers adds roughly 24 pages, taking the block from 120 to 144: eighteen signatures of eight, or nine of sixteen. It also fixes the object. At 120 pages on 120 gsm the spine is about 11 mm, which is thin for a casebound annual at this trim. At 144 pages on 150 gsm it is closer to 17 mm, which is a book.

A1   Text spread, two column

A1 — the default

Text spread, two column

Two columns of 39 per cent measure with a 9 mm gutter, justified, hyphenated on the Oxford dictionary. Body Newsreader 400 at 9.5 / 13 pt. Subheads Newsreader 400 at 13 pt with keep-with-next enforced.

  • Verso running head is the chapter, Geist Mono 7 pt. Recto is the publication, Newsreader italic 8 pt.
  • Nothing spans both columns. Chrome balances rather than fills a fragmented multicol, so any spanner strands a short row above it.
  • Hard ceiling of 850 words. Above that the page gets a figure, a pull, or a break.
Frequency: the majority of the block, but never more than four in a row

A2   Text page with column furniture

A2 — furniture at column measure

Text page with column furniture

Images, pull quotes and editor's notes sit inside a single column. They never span. One furniture item per page, two per spread, and never two of the same kind on facing pages.

  • Images at 26 to 34 per cent of page height, always with a caption.
  • Pull quotes Newsreader 300 italic 18 / 22 pt on a 0.6 mm marine rule. One per spread. The August proof puts three on folio 29 alone; that page is the worst offender in the book.
  • The pull quote must be a claim the reader can disagree with, not a decorative restatement of the sentence beside it.
Frequency: roughly every third page of running text

A3   Tall figure  ·  A4   Half-bleed

A3 / A4 — scale variety

Tall figure and half-bleed

The proof has exactly two image scales: full-bleed and column-width. Everything in between is missing, which is why the supporting pictures read as drop-ins rather than as art direction.

  • A3, tall figure. One column wide, half to two-thirds of page height, sat at the head of the column so the caption falls on the optical centre line.
  • A4, half-bleed. Image runs off the outer, head and foot trim; text holds the inner column only. Use once per chapter, at the hinge between the argument and the evidence.
  • Half-bleed pages carry no running head. The folio stays.
Frequency: A3 once per chapter, A4 once per chapter

B2   Gutter-crossing spread

B2 — the one big picture

Gutter-crossing image spread

A single photograph across both pages, no type except a reversed caption in the lower outer corner of the verso. This is the most expensive gesture in the book and the least repeatable: once per chapter at the very most, and only where the picture carries an argument the text cannot.

  • Nothing important crosses the gutter. Faces, horizons and hulls sit clear of the fold by 12 mm each side.
  • Needs a 5,600 px master. The present library tops out at 2,700 px, which is 291 dpi on a single page and 146 dpi across a spread. Every spread image has to be re-sourced or commissioned.
Frequency: three to four in the book, not nine

B3   Number page  ·  B4   Quiet page

B3 / B4 — the two new relief pages

Number page and quiet page

B3, the number page. Three to five figures set in Newsreader 300 at 96 to 120 pt in marine, each with an italic gloss of no more than fifteen words. This is the single device the Wealth Report uses most and the Reference uses not at all, and it is the cheapest relief in publishing: it needs no photograph, no permission and no commission, only a fact the publication already owns.

B4, the quiet page. One sentence, centred on the optical third, attributed, with the chapter numeral watermarked at seven per cent. Used where a chapter turns, not where a chapter ends.

  • Numbers on B3 must be sourced in the back matter like any other claim.
  • B4 carries no folio and no running head.
Frequency: B3 once per chapter, B4 four or five in the book

B5   Chapter close (existing, keep)

B5 — keep as built

Chapter close

The composed full page at the end of each chapter is the strongest piece of design already in the proof and it stays exactly as it is: At a glance, the closing pull, the Read next line, the section mark, the watermarked numeral, foot pinned by auto margin.

  • Minimum height 240 mm, no forced break, so a short case tail can be absorbed above it.
  • The watermark sits at bottom: 0. A negative offset paints it on the following page.

C1   Full-measure figure  ·  C2   Table page

C1 / C2 — charts and tables

The data spread, rebuilt

Across the nine data spreads there are 32 tables and 14 charts. That ratio is backwards for print. A table is a lookup device and the reader of a printed annual is not looking anything up; they are being shown a shape. Convert until charts and tables are near parity.

  • Charts print at full type measure and at least 40 per cent of page height. In the proof, figure 01.03 occupies 22 per cent of its page and leaves 45 per cent of the page empty below it. Axis labels currently set around 5 pt; the floor is 7 pt.
  • Tables keep hairlines at head and foot and a 0.5 mm marine rule under the head row only. No vertical rules, no zebra. Tabular oldstyle figures, decimal-aligned, ranges on en-dashes.
  • Captions carry the finding, not the restatement. "Figure 01.01: operating cost by size band" tells the reader nothing they cannot see. The caption should say what the chart proves.
  • One form per dataset. If a table is followed by a chart of the same numbers, the print build drops the table.
Frequency: two to three pages per chapter, opening on a verso

D4   Checklist  ·  back matter measure

D4 — the page that gets used

Checklist and back matter

The checklists are the most practically valuable thing the publication prints and the proof buries them inside the chapters at body measure. In the book they gather into a single section, Questions to ask, set one column at 60 per cent measure, numbered in marine, with 18 pt leading and a rule under the section head.

  • Ranged left, never justified. A checklist read aloud in a meeting should not have rivers in it.
  • One question per numbered item, no sub-clauses, no more than two lines each.
  • Glossary and sources return to two column at body measure; the index goes to three at 7.5 pt.
  • Every back-matter section opens on a recto.
Frequency: 24 pages of back matter, opening after chapter 09

D1 — revised opener

Chapter opener spread

The proof already forces every opener onto a verso via spacer pages. The change is that the numeral and the title now separate: the numeral belongs to the image, the title belongs to the type page, and the two read as one composition across the fold rather than as a picture with words on it.

  • The coordinates line goes, unless every one of the nine is verified against its actual photograph. Three are currently wrong. D2   Case study opener  ·  D3 pattern follows for guest Q&A

D2 / D3 / D4 — case, Q&A, checklist

Case study, guest opinion, checklist

D2, case study. Title block and the case photograph on the verso, body running two column across the recto. The disclosure line is part of the design, not an apology at the foot: it is the thing the trade press cannot print.

D3, guest Q&A. Portrait at 22 mm square on the opener, questions in marine 9.5 pt, answers in charcoal, two column, fragmenting freely. Six of the nine chapters have a contributor. The three without cannot show an empty slot; they take a B3 number page instead.

D4, checklist. Single column at 60 per cent measure, numbered, generous leading, ranged left. This is the page readers photograph and send to their lawyer. Design it to be photographed.

Frequency: nine cases, six Q&As, nine checklists

E  ·  Chapter architecture

How the registers assemble into a chapter

Sixteen pages, one signature, the same shape every time. The reader learns the shape by chapter two and can then navigate the book without the contents page.

Where the checklist goes. The nine checklists currently sit inside the chapters. In print they should gather into a single back-matter section, Questions to ask, immediately before the glossary. On the web they belong in the chapter because a reader arrives at one chapter from search. In the book they belong together because a reader who has finished the book wants the whole apparatus in one place, and because a 16-page section of checklists is the part of the object that gets used rather than read.

Where the sequence is allowed to break. Chapter 09 is a decision framework, not an argument with evidence; it takes no case study and no data spread, and runs eight pages. Chapters 05 and 07 are the two longest in the manuscript and take twenty-four pages each, which means a second data spread and a second number page rather than longer runs of text.

Front matter, 10 pages. Half-title, frontispiece, title, imprint and masthead, editors' letter, contents (two pages), contributors, a full-bleed. Back matter, 24 pages. Questions to ask, glossary, sources, index, colophon, closing image, numbered plate.

The arithmetic. Nine chapters averaging sixteen pages is 144, plus 34 pages of front and back matter is 178, which overshoots. Holding the block at 144 means the chapter template runs at fourteen pages for six chapters and twenty for the three long ones, with front and back matter compressed to 24. The final call belongs to the copy-fit pass, not to this document; what does not move is the ratio of relief to text.
