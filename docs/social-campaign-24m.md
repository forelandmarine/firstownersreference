# Social campaign: the first owner at 24 metres and above

Drafted 2026-07-31. A focused campaign layer inside the 1st Edition launch, aimed squarely at the newly-liquid principal buying, or about to buy, a yacht at or above 24 metres, and at the advisers who sit next to that decision. Sits under `marketing-plan.md` and inherits every rule in `social-strategy.md`. The day-by-day schedule is in `social-calendar.md`. Cards are produced in the [social card studio](https://firstownersreference-social.vercel.app).

## Why 24 metres

Twenty-four metres is the line where yacht ownership changes character. It is the load-line length at which the commercial codes bite, the point where crew stop being optional and become a payroll, and roughly where first-time buyers enter the market the publication is written for. Below it, a capable owner can run the boat. At and above it, the owner is running a small business with a floating asset at the centre of it, and almost no first-time buyer is told that plainly before they sign.

The campaign speaks to the person at that threshold. Not the seasoned repeat owner, who has already learned this at cost. The first one.

## Who we are speaking to

Three rings, one target.

The principal, at the centre. A first-time buyer looking at 24 to 60 metres, frequently newly liquid from a sale, a liquidity event, or an inheritance. Intelligent, time-poor, and being courted by everyone with a commission attached. Reads LinkedIn between meetings. Has been handed a lot of confident advice and has no independent way to weigh it.

The adviser, one ring out. Family-office principals and staff, private-client lawyers, wealth managers. They will not buy a yacht, but they are asked about one, and they forward what is credible. The publication is written to be forwardable. Much of the campaign reaches the principal through the adviser's inbox, not directly.

The practitioner, the outer ring. Captains, surveyors, managers. They amplify, they correct, and their engagement is the legitimacy signal that makes the adviser take the publication seriously. We do not sell to them. We earn their citation.

The campaign is designed for the principal and pitched at the register the adviser will pass on.

## What the first owner is actually deciding

Each decision maps to a chapter and to a place the folklore costs real money. This is the spine of the campaign.

Running cost. The 10 percent rule has no traceable origin and the working range is 8 to 20 percent. A principal who budgets on the folklore is wrong by millions over a hold period. Chapter 01.

The market they are entering. The order book has fallen for a second year to 1,093 yachts. Supply is narrowing, which changes slot availability and negotiating position for a first buyer. Chapter 02.

Who is paid by whom. A buyer's broker paid by commission on the boat is a structural impossibility. Dual agency, retrocessions, and the captain's position sit at the centre of the acquisition. Chapter 03.

The process itself. Survey, sea trial, MOA, VAT regime, flag. Five pitfalls that recur. Chapter 04.

New build against brokerage. A 24 to 36 month commitment against immediacy, and the owner's representative question. Chapter 05.

Refit. Projects run 30 to 50 percent over quoted scope as a rule, not an exception. When to refit and when to sell. Chapter 06.

Operations. Hire the captain first and hire slowly. Crew is 30 to 40 percent of annual cost, and the charter economics almost never work. Chapter 07.

Motor against sail. A sailing programme at 50 metres runs meaningfully below the equivalent motor yacht, and few first buyers spend enough time on the decision. Chapter 08.

The decision framework. Ten questions to put to anyone advising on the purchase, the publisher included. Chapter 09.

## Message architecture

Three layers, in this order.

The wedge is independence. Every post carries, explicitly or by implication, the fact that the publication is paid by no one it writes about. This is the single thing no broker, yard, or management company can say, and it is why the material is worth a principal's attention.

The proof is the sourced figure. Nothing is asserted without a named source and a year. The credibility architecture is the differentiator, not the phrasing. A principal who has been sold to all week recognises the difference immediately.

The utility is the tool. Each post has one conversion point: the running cost calculator, the VAT tool, a chapter, the ten questions, or a print request. The campaign gives the principal something to do that helps them, not something to buy.

## Channels, for this audience

Unchanged from `social-strategy.md`, restated for the principal.

LinkedIn converts. It is where the principal and the adviser both are, daily, and where a source-cited card reads as a break from the feed. The publication page carries the campaign. The two editors' personal accounts carry the highest leverage because the platform rewards people over pages, and because a principal trusts a named practitioner over a logo.

Instagram follows. It holds the type-led, counter-aesthetic presence that a principal checks at the weekend. It does not convert and is not asked to. No drone shots, no champagne, no sunsets. The job is to look like the publication, at smaller scale.

## Guardrails, non-negotiable

All of `social-strategy.md` applies. The ones this campaign is most likely to strain, because it is aimed at a high-value buyer, are worth restating.

No broker or yard tags, ever, including in any post that celebrates a purchase decision. No paid amplification, no matter how tempting the audience precision. No product placement and no featured yacht. No engagement bait, including the softer forms that creep in when chasing a valuable reader. Source and year on every figure. If a post cannot carry its source, it does not run.

## What this layer is measured on

Inside the launch scorecard in `marketing-plan.md`, this campaign is read on four lines.

Print copy requests from the named +24m distribution ring. Calculator and VAT-tool runs attributable to social. Inbound from advisers, tagged at `editors@firstownersreference.com`. Named-source citation, the long-term legitimacy signal. Follower count is reported and not steered toward.
